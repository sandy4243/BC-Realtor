## Why a licence is more than a permission slip

> **Scenario.** Six months after you're licensed, three things happen in one week:
>
> 1. Your cousin asks you to "just help" lease out his retail unit in Surrey. He'll pay you $2,000 cash when a tenant signs.
> 2. A developer you met at a networking event offers you $5,000 a month, paid straight to you, to staff the sales centre for his new industrial strata project.
> 3. Your assistant, who isn't licensed, offers to run Saturday's open house so you can attend a family wedding.
>
> All three sound reasonable. **All three, done the way they were offered, would break the law that governs your licence.**

That law is the **Real Estate Services Act (RESA)**. It decides:

- what activities need a licence,
- who's exempt,
- what kind of licence you hold and what it lets you do,
- who regulates you, and
- what protections exist for the public if a licensee gets it wrong.

This lesson is the first half of RESA: **who can do what, and who's watching.** Lesson 5 covers the second half: money in trust, the day-to-day Rules, discipline, and pre-sale developments.

> 🧭 **Exam note.** The course says you don't need to memorize section numbers. Focus on what each rule **does**. A few numbers come up so often, though (like s.3, the licensing requirement), that they're worth knowing.

## Vocabulary first

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **RESA** | The *Real Estate Services Act*: the BC statute that licenses and regulates real estate professionals. In force since early 2005. |
| **Regulation** | The *Real Estate Services Regulation*, made by Cabinet under RESA. Has the force of law. Covers exemptions, fees, trust accounts and more. |
| **Rules** | The *Real Estate Services Rules*, made by **BCFSA** under RESA. The day-to-day rulebook for licensees. Most discipline cases are about breaking a Rule. |
| **BCFSA** | BC Financial Services Authority: the provincial regulator for real estate licensees (and credit unions, insurers, mortgage brokers and more). |
| **Superintendent of Real Estate** | BCFSA's chief executive officer, who holds the key legal powers under RESA and delegates most of them to BCFSA staff. |
| **Licensee** | Anyone licensed under RESA: a brokerage, managing broker, associate broker or representative. |
| **Brokerage** | The licensed firm. **Clients contract with the brokerage**, not with the individual agent. |
| **Representative** | The entry-level individual licence. What you'll hold first. |
| **Remuneration** | Payment of any kind: commission, fee, bonus, gift in return for services. |
| **Principal** | The person a licensee acts for (the client). |
| **Fiduciary** | Someone in a position of trust who must act **only** in the other person's best interests. |
| **Exemption** | A situation where someone can provide real estate services for pay **without** a licence. |

## Where RESA came from (and why it keeps getting tougher)

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| **2004–2005** | RESA and its companion, the *Real Estate Development Marketing Act* (REDMA), replace the old *Real Estate Act*. |
| **2016** | After public concern about industry practices, rule-making power shifts from the Real Estate Council (RECBC) to the **Superintendent**. Maximum penalties for individuals jump **25 times**, from $10,000 to **$250,000**. |
| **2021** | RECBC and the Office of the Superintendent are folded into **BCFSA**, creating a **single regulator** for BC's financial services sector. BCFSA now makes the Rules, through a more formal process. |

The direction is clear: **more oversight, bigger penalties, more consumer protection.** You're entering a profession the public and government are watching closely.

### Three layers of rules

```
RESA (the statute, passed by the legislature)
   ├── Real Estate Services Regulation  ← made by Cabinet; force of law
   └── Real Estate Services Rules       ← made by BCFSA; the day-to-day rulebook
```

You'll find all three on **bclaws.ca**. When in doubt, read the actual wording; exemptions in particular often come with conditions.

### What RESA is for

RESA's purpose is to **protect the public** by:

1. making sure licensees meet **minimum competency** standards before they're licensed,
2. setting **conduct standards** while they're licensed, and
3. appointing a **regulator** (BCFSA, with the Superintendent) to enforce it all.

## What needs a licence: RESA s.3

The core rule:

> You must not provide **real estate services** **to or on behalf of another**, **for or in expectation of remuneration**, unless you're **licensed** or **exempt**.

Three tests, all of which must be met for s.3 to bite:

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Real estate services** | Is it trading, rental property management or strata management? |
| **To or on behalf of another** | Are you doing it for someone else? |
| **For or in expectation of remuneration** | Are you being paid, or expecting to be? |

### The three kinds of real estate services

**1. Trading services**: services for a party to a **trade in real estate**:

- advising on the right **price**,
- making **representations** about the property,
- **finding** a property for someone to acquire,
- **finding** someone to acquire a property,
- **showing** it,
- **negotiating** price or terms,
- **presenting offers**, and
- **receiving deposit** money.

A "**trade in real estate**" is wider than a sale. It includes:

- buying, selling or **leasing** real estate (or any other acquisition or disposition),
- **assigning** a contract of purchase, sale or lease, and
- a **prospective** trade (one that hasn't happened yet).

And "**real estate**" includes not just land but **cooperative interests, shared interests in land and time share interests**, even where these are technically shares or contract rights rather than interests in land.

> 🏢 **Commercial angle.** Leasing is a trade in real estate. Negotiating a 10-year lease for a warehouse tenant, or assigning a lease when a business is sold, is **trading services** and needs a licence. Much of commercial practice is leasing.

**2. Rental property management services**, for an owner of rental real estate:

- trading services related to **renting** the property,
- **collecting rent** or security deposits, and
- **managing** it: paying third parties, negotiating or signing contracts, supervising contractors or staff, handling landlord–tenant matters.

**3. Strata management services**, for a strata corporation:

- collecting or holding **strata fees**, contributions and levies, and
- exercising the strata's or council's **delegated powers**: paying third parties, signing contracts, supervising contractors, **enforcing bylaws**.

## "Once a licensee, always a licensee"

Once you're licensed, RESA covers **all** your real estate services, even when you:

- act **for yourself** (selling your own condo),
- act for someone else **for free** (helping a friend), or
- would otherwise fall under an **exemption**.

**Rule of thumb:** every real estate service you provide must go **through your brokerage** and be **in its name** (RESA s.7). You can't be paid for real estate services by anyone except your brokerage.

> **Example: selling your own property.** Annette, a licensee, wants to sell her own house. If she markets or shows it herself, that's trading services, so it must go **through her brokerage**, and her ads must show the brokerage name. But it's risky:
>
> - transactions where the licensee has an **ownership interest aren't covered** by the mandatory E&O insurance;
> - an unrepresented buyer may later claim she used her expertise against them; and
> - she must give the buyer a written **disclosure of her interest** (Lesson 9).
>
> **Better:** have **another licensee** in her brokerage (or another brokerage) list it, and stay out of direct contact with the buyer and buyer's agent.

> 🚨 **Case: "just helping a friend."** A trading licensee helped an overseas friend rent out a BC house, took rent cheques, dealt with tree and plumbing complaints, and was paid directly by the friend. Problems: she wasn't licensed for **rental property management**, and she worked **outside her brokerage**. Result: **$7,500** penalty, **$1,500** enforcement costs, and a remedial course.

**Other jobs are fine; other real estate jobs on the side are not.** You can't independently work for a developer, as an appraiser or as a caretaker outside your brokerage, and you can't be paid directly by a company for anything that counts as real estate services. If you have other employment, review it with your managing broker **before** applying; BCFSA's Practice Standards Advisors can also answer questions in confidence.

**Back to the scenario:**

- 🏪 **Cousin's lease:** that's trading services for pay. It must go **through your brokerage**, with any fee paid **to the brokerage**, and documented like any other file.
- 🏗️ **Developer's offer:** you can't be **paid directly** by the developer for real estate services. The arrangement must run through your brokerage.

## Exemptions: who doesn't need a licence

Some people can provide real estate services for pay without a licence. Exemptions come from three places.

### In RESA itself (s.3)

| Exempt person | Scope |
|---|---|
| A person acting under **court authority** | |
| **Trustee in bankruptcy, receiver, liquidator** (appointed under statute) | In that capacity |
| **Executor or administrator** of an estate | For the estate's real estate |
| **Trustee** under a will, marriage settlement or deed of trust | Under that trust |
| **Trust company** with a trust business authorization | For real estate it owns, holds or administers |
| **Practising lawyer** | In the course of their practice |

### In the Regulation

**General exemptions** (four groups): **employees** of a principal acting for that principal; **collection agents**; certain **mining and oil and gas** activities; **governments** and government corporations.

**Trading services exemptions** (eight groups):

1. **Employees of developers** (for that developer's development units only)
2. **Notaries**
3. **Accountants**, for the purchase and sale of a **business**
4. **Appraisers and property inspectors**
5. **Auctioneers**
6. People giving **general information** to help owners sell their own property, or publishing ads
7. People who **refer** others to a licensee or exempt person
8. **Agents of expropriating authorities**

> ⚠️ **Developer's employees** must tell buyers **in writing**, promptly and **before any agreement**, that they're **not licensed**, are **employed by the developer**, and act for the **developer**, not the buyer. The disclosure must be **separate** from the purchase agreement and from any REDMA disclosure statement.

**Rental property management exemptions** include caretakers and managers employed by owners, certain brokerage caretakers doing limited tasks, people managing BC Housing properties, and lenders or mortgage brokers acting under an **assignment of rents**.

**Strata management exemptions** include **strata lot owners** acting as owners, caretakers or managers of the strata, and **owner developers**.

### In the Rules (for licensees)

- **ss.94–95:** a licensee can manage rental property they **own**, or that's owned by their **spouse, children or parents**, outside the brokerage, if they meet the conditions (Lesson 5).
- **s.96:** a licensee who **owns a strata lot** can sit on **strata council** as a volunteer, without RESA applying, if they:
  - **disclose in writing** to the strata corporation that they're not acting as a licensee and the strata doesn't get RESA's protections (BCFSA has a form for this),
  - give a **copy to their managing broker**,
  - don't have **sole signing authority** over strata funds, and
  - aren't **paid**.

## Unlicensed assistants

You **can** hire unlicensed help, and many successful licensees do. But you **can't pay an unlicensed person** for anything that requires a licence (Rules s.66), and you're responsible for **supervising** them (Rules s.29).

| ✅ An unlicensed assistant **may** | ❌ An unlicensed assistant **may not** |
|---|---|
| Fill in contract forms **at your direction and with your approval** | **Host an open house**, or solicit buyers or sellers in any way |
| Answer phones, take messages, forward calls | **Show** a property to a client or buyer (*exception*: a property manager's assistant can show rentals to prospective tenants) |
| Filing and bookkeeping | **Advise** a consumer about a contract |
| Draft documents and letters **for your signature** | **Present or negotiate** an offer |
| Put up and take down **signs** | **Communicate with consumers** about a transaction |
| **Witness** signatures | |
| Arrange **repairs** on a rental | |

> 🚨 **Case.** A buyer's agent let her unlicensed assistant book and attend two showings, then had the brokerage pay the assistant her **entire** commission share. On another deal, the assistant advised the buyer and talked to the seller's agent about the offer. Result (with other breaches): **licence suspended**, a substantial penalty and a remedial course.

**Back to the scenario:** your assistant **can't host the open house.** Find a licensed colleague in your brokerage to cover it, or reschedule.

## You're an agent, and that means fiduciary

RESA s.3 is about acting **"to or on behalf of another"**. That's **agency**: one person (the **agent**) authorized to act for another (the **principal**) in dealing with third parties. Lesson 9 covers it in depth, but you need the basics from day one.

**Why people hire agents:** to sign while they're away, to save time, but mainly for the agent's **knowledge and skill** in a complex, stressful, expensive process.

Because clients trust agents with so much, the law treats real estate agency as a **fiduciary relationship**, like doctor–patient or lawyer–client. The core fiduciary duties:

| Duty | In practice |
|---|---|
| **Loyalty** | Act **solely** for your client's benefit, even at a cost to yourself. |
| **Avoid conflicts of interest** | Don't put yourself where your interests compete with your client's. |
| **Full disclosure** | Tell your client everything relevant you know. |
| **Confidentiality** | Protect your client's private information. |
| **Obey lawful instructions** | Do what your client lawfully asks, within your authority. |

> 🧠 **The one question to ask, every time:** *"Is this action in my client's best interests?"* The whole licensing system exists to make sure licensees answer it honestly.

## Who regulates you

### BCFSA

BC Financial Services Authority is a **Crown agency** of the BC government. It regulates:

- **real estate licensees** and **unlicensed** real estate activity,
- **real estate development marketing** (REDMA),
- mortgage brokers, credit unions, pension plans, trust companies and insurance companies, and
- the Credit Union Deposit Insurance Corporation.

Its main goal in real estate: **strengthen consumer protection**, so the public can trust licensed professionals, and stop unlicensed people providing services.

### The Superintendent of Real Estate

The Superintendent is **BCFSA's chief executive officer** (appointed by BCFSA's board), and also holds other titles such as Superintendent of Financial Institutions and Registrar of Mortgage Brokers. RESA gives the Superintendent key powers: **licensing**, **investigations** and **discipline**. With 25,000+ licensees, the Superintendent **delegates** most of these powers to BCFSA staff. That's why the course (and this site) just says "BCFSA".

## Three bodies that protect the public (and you)

### 1. Real Estate Foundation of BC (REFBC)

- Created in **1985**; a non-profit run by a **Board of Governors** (3 appointed by the province, and 1 each by the Union of BC Municipalities, BCREA, REIBC and the BC Non-Profit Housing Association). Three-year terms; no more than six consecutive years.
- **Funded by the interest on brokerage trust accounts**, except:
  - separate trust accounts set up **for specific clients**, and
  - accounts holding tenants' **security or pet damage deposits** (that interest goes to the tenant under the *Residential Tenancy Act*).
- Gives **grants** for land use, built environments, fresh water, food sovereignty and the real estate profession, and supports reconciliation with Indigenous Peoples.

### 2. Real Estate Errors and Omissions Insurance Corporation

Created in **1988**. Runs the **mandatory** professional liability insurance (the **Indemnity Plan**) that every licensee must carry. It protects the public from an **"empty judgment"**: winning a lawsuit against a licensee who can't pay.

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Limit** | **$2 million** per licence, **including** defence costs |
| **Defence costs** | Paid by the insurer, which can **settle without your consent** |
| **Deductible** | **$2,000 per licensee** when damages are paid (licensee + brokerage both sued = $4,000). **No deductible** if only defence costs are paid ("first dollar defence") |
| **Premium** | **$315/year** ($630 per two-year licence), collected by BCFSA with your licence fees. PRECs don't pay a separate premium. |
| **Basis** | **Claims made**: the claim must be **reported during the policy term** to be covered |
| **Territory** | Claims anywhere, if made and pursued **in Canada** |

**Key exclusions** (not covered):

1. Acting as an insurance agent, builder, contractor, **developer**, **mortgagee**, notary, appraiser or court-appointed administrator
2. Deals where you **have or may get an ownership interest**
3. **Commission or fee disputes**
4. **Bodily injury**, sickness or death
5. **Dishonest, fraudulent, criminal or malicious** acts
6. **Human Rights Code** discrimination claims
7. **Residential Tenancy Act** claims

> 🚨 **ALERT: E&O doesn't pay BCFSA penalties.** A licensee read only **page one** of a title and missed that half the property was held by an insolvency trustee, so they acted without proper authority. Penalty **$8,500** plus **$1,250** in costs, **all out of pocket**. No fine, sanction or penalty from BCFSA is insured under the plan.

> 🏢 **Commercial angle: is $2 million enough?** Often not. Commercial and high-value deals can produce claims well over $2M, and defence costs eat into the limit. Selling many similar units (a strata project, a portfolio) can also produce **related claims** sharing **one** limit. Commercial licensees commonly buy **excess E&O**, plus:
> - **cyber** insurance (data breaches),
> - **commercial general liability** (someone hurt at a showing; Lesson 2's Occupiers Liability Act),
> - coverage for **disciplinary defence costs and penalties**, and
> - **property / business interruption** insurance.
>
> Many brokerages arrange excess coverage. Ask about yours, and talk to an insurance broker.

### 3. Real Estate Compensation Fund Corporation

- Board: **3 directors appointed by BCFSA, 2 by BCREA**.
- Runs the **Special Compensation Fund**, funded by licensee fees.
- Compensates people who lose money because a licensee (or brokerage officer, director, employee or contractor) **misappropriated** it, **intentionally failed to pay or account** for it, or got it by **fraud**.
- **Not** for licensees claiming unpaid commission.
- Claimant applies **in writing** to BCFSA; time limits apply. BCFSA may hold a hearing or leave it to the courts. A court's final assessment or a BCFSA discipline finding of wrongful taking is **binding** on the assessment.
- Maximums: **$200,000 per claimant** and **$1 million per brokerage**.
- A licensee who gets notice of a related court case or discipline hearing must **immediately notify** BCFSA and the Compensation Fund Corporation in writing.

> **Case.** A licensee asked buyers for repeated "deposits", supposedly to go towards their down payments. When the purchases fell through, he returned only part of the money. The buyers were awarded **$95,000** from the fund. The licensee was also convicted of fraud, ordered to pay **$172,352** restitution, and suspended.

> 🧠 **Don't mix them up:**
> - **E&O insurance** → your **negligent mistakes** (careless, honest errors).
> - **Compensation Fund** → licensee **theft or fraud** with the client's money.
> - **Foundation** → gets the **interest** on trust accounts and funds public-good projects.

## The licensing system

### Four levels of licence

| Level | Who | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Brokerage** | The firm (or an individual sole proprietor) | Provides services **only through** its licensed individuals. **Contracts are with the brokerage.** Must have a **managing broker**, show **sound finances**, keep records **in BC for 7 years**, and file annual statements within **120 days** of year-end. |
| **Managing broker** | Runs the brokerage | Acts for the brokerage for all RESA purposes; responsible for its conduct and for **supervising** everyone. |
| **Associate broker** | Qualified as a managing broker, but working **under** one | A ready backup if the managing broker can't act. |
| **Representative** | Works under a managing broker | **Most licensees.** Your starting licence. |

Only a **brokerage** licence can be held by a partnership or corporation (other than a **personal real estate corporation**, below).

**The single-brokerage rule (RESA s.7):** individuals must be licensed to, and engaged by, **one** brokerage only, must provide services **only** on its behalf, and can accept pay **only** from it. (Exception: a managing broker can be licensed to up to **four affiliated** brokerages.)

**Vicarious liability.** You're personally liable for your own wrongs, but your **brokerage can also be sued** for what you do on its behalf, even if it didn't know. That's why brokerages want problems reported **immediately**.

**Training and supervision** of licensees can only be done by someone who is a **licensee of the brokerage** (RESA s.7.1), not by an unlicensed owner or director.

### Managing broker duties (Rules s.28)

- Be **actively engaged** in managing the brokerage; make sure business is done competently and lawfully; ensure **adequate supervision** of all licensees and staff.
- **Deal with misconduct** they know about.
- Keep **trust accounts and records** in order.
- Make sure all parties are **immediately notified, in writing**, if a **deposit isn't received** or a deposit cheque **bounces**.

### Your duties as a representative (Rules s.29)

- Give your managing broker **copies of all records** (disclosures, service agreements, offers, contracts, accounting statements).
- **Keep them informed** of your activities, and tell them **immediately** if a deposit isn't received.
- **Respond promptly** to their inquiries.
- **Supervise** your own staff and assistants.
- **Report misconduct promptly**: your own, your staff's, and other licensees' in your brokerage.

> 🚨 **ALERT: you must report misconduct.** Deceptive dealing, misappropriation, unauthorized signing and secret commissions must be reported, and so should "small" things: BCFSA investigations into trivial issues often uncover serious ones. Ask yourself:
> - Were conduct requirements breached?
> - Was a consumer harmed, or could someone be harmed if it's repeated?
> - Does the licensee benefit, and was that disclosed?
> - Do they seem unaware or unwilling to fix it?
>
> Any "yes" → tell your managing broker. You can also use **BCFSA's anonymous tipline** (include what happened, when and where, and who).

### Personal real estate corporations (PREC)

Under the Regulation, a licensee can be licensed **through a corporation**, mainly for **tax and income planning**.

- One director, one voting shareholder and one president, **all the same person** (the "controlling individual").
- **No liability shield**: you're still personally liable to third parties.
- Misconduct by the PREC is treated as misconduct by you, and vice versa.

### Teams

Teams aren't mentioned in RESA, but the Rules regulate them. Two or more licensees **must register with BCFSA as a team** if, in providing trading services, they:

- present themselves to the public **as a single entity**,
- are regularly **designated agents of the same client**, or
- regularly work together in a way that makes them look like **implied agents** of the same party.

**Team rules:**

- Managing broker must **approve** joining or registering (but not leaving).
- **One team at a time**; all members at the **same brokerage**.
- **Unlicensed assistants can't be registered** as members (an application including one is rejected), and any ad naming them must say they're unlicensed.
- Ads must show the **team name** and the **brokerage name**.
- **Trading services only.** No rental or strata management through a team, and a team can't advertise those services.
- The whole team is the **designated agent** for every team client. So one member **can't act for the seller while another acts for the buyer** in the same deal. Contracts list **every member's name** (not the team name), though any member can sign.
- Every member keeps **full personal responsibility** under RESA, whoever the "lead" is.

> 💡 **Should a new licensee join a team?** Pros: an established referral network, mentorship, shared marketing and admin. Cons: commission splits. It's a business decision, not a requirement.

### Categories of licence

Within each level, your **category** depends on which services you're licensed for. **Seven** categories:

| Trading | Rental PM | Strata mgmt |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ | | |
| | ✅ | |
| | | ✅ |
| ✅ | ✅ | |
| ✅ | | ✅ |
| | ✅ | ✅ |
| ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

**The managing broker's category caps the brokerage.** If the managing broker is licensed for trading and rental PM only, nobody at the brokerage can provide strata management.

## Getting and keeping your licence

### Qualifications (RESA s.10)

An applicant must show:

1. **Good reputation** and **suitability** for the level and category.
2. For individuals: at least **19 years old**, and meet the **education and experience** requirements.
3. For partnerships and corporations: partners, directors and officers are of good reputation.
4. No refusal, discipline, suspension, cancellation, professional discipline or **conviction** (in real estate, insurance, mortgage broking or securities, here or elsewhere) **for a reason that shows them unfit**.

### Good reputation

- An **original criminal record check** dated within **90 days** of BCFSA receiving the application (or an online check through **Triton Canada**).
- Application questions on charges, convictions, bankruptcy, judgments, and refusals or discipline elsewhere.
- **Concealing** information destroys the presumption of good reputation. Disclose fully, even old or minor matters (impaired driving is the most common). Incomplete answers cause long delays.
- If there's doubt, BCFSA may hold a **qualification hearing**.

> **Case.** An applicant's mortgage broker licence had been cancelled for submitting falsified tax documents. He disclosed it honestly, showed remorse, explained his naïvety at the time, and provided strong references. **Licensed, with extra supervision conditions.** Rehabilitation and honesty count.

### Education

- **Representatives:** pass the licensing course and exam for each service type (trading, rental PM, strata), then complete an **Applied Practice Course**: **Residential** or **Commercial** Trading Services (about **five months**: online, classroom and field assignments). Apply for your licence once you start; a **temporary licence** issues after the first two or three weeks. Miss the deadline and it's **terminated**.
- **English proficiency:** **CELPIP-General level 7** in reading, writing, speaking and listening, unless you have a degree from an English-language institution or were licensed elsewhere in Canada under an English requirement.
- **Associate and managing brokers:** representative education **plus** the **Broker's Licensing Course** and exam.
- Requirements can be **waived** for previous education or training.

> 🏢 **Your path.** As a future commercial specialist, you'll pass **this** licensing course, then take the **Commercial Trading Services Applied Practice Course**. There's no separate commercial licence; the specialization starts with that post-licensing course.

### Experience

- **Representatives:** none.
- **Associate or managing broker** (first time): at least **2 years** as a licensee in the **past 5 years** (BC or equivalent). Can be waived; for managing brokers, also where there's an **urgent local need**.

### Term, renewal and status

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Term** | **2 years** |
| **Temporary licence** | Has conditions and a deadline; **cancelled automatically** if not met |
| **Renewal** | Apply **at least 30 days** before expiry. If you apply on time, your licence **continues** until BCFSA decides. Complete BCFSA's **mandatory continuing education** first (BCREA members also need their PDP). |
| **Refusal** | For unsuitability, missing requirements or unfitness. BCFSA must give you a **chance to be heard**, then **written reasons** and notice of your right to appeal to the **Financial Services Tribunal**. Brokerage names likely to **confuse or mislead** can be refused. |
| **Inoperative** | Your licence **automatically stops working** if you leave your brokerage, until you join another. If the **managing broker's** licence becomes inoperative, suspended or cancelled, the **brokerage's** does too, and so do **everyone's** at that brokerage. (That's why brokerages keep backup brokers.) |
| **Surrender** | You can voluntarily surrender; it becomes inoperative unless reinstated during the term. |

### Keep BCFSA informed (Rules s.23)

You must **promptly notify BCFSA in writing** (and copy your managing broker) if:

- you're subject to **discipline or regulatory proceedings** in real estate, insurance, securities, or for mortgage brokers, accountants, notaries or lawyers (anywhere);
- you have a **court order or judgment** against you relating to real estate services, insurance, mortgages or securities, or **fraud, misappropriation or breach of trust**;
- you're in **bankruptcy, insolvency or receivership**;
- a business you own (or were a director, officer or partner of in the **last 2 years**) has such a judgment or insolvency; or
- you're **charged with or convicted of** an offence (excluding minor traffic and violation tickets).

> 🚨 **Charged, not just convicted.** One licensee thought he only had to report **convictions**, not **charges**: reprimand plus **$1,500** costs. In 2024, another failed to report two major *Motor Vehicle Act* driving charges: **$5,000** in penalties.

### Names, advertising and where you work

- Your **licensee name** is your legal name (or a recognizable short form) or one BCFSA approves.
- You must clearly show your name **and your brokerage's name** when providing services, and every ad must show the **brokerage name prominently** (Rules ss.39–40).
- **Employee or independent contractor**, your **duties are identical**. The difference is mainly tax and employment law.
- **Working from a personal office** (Rules s.37):
  1. **no sign** visible outside saying real estate services are provided there,
  2. **don't answer the phone** in the brokerage's name, and
  3. **don't put the personal office address** on ads or records.

## Two alerts

> 🚨 **ALERT 1: Everything through the brokerage.** Every real estate service, every dollar of remuneration, every record. No side deals, no direct payments, even for family.

> 🚨 **ALERT 2: Know your licence's limits.** A trading licence doesn't let you manage rentals. A team can't do property management. An unlicensed assistant can't show or host. When in doubt, ask your managing broker before you act.

## Trap list: things the exam likes to twist

1. s.3 needs **all three**: real estate services + for another + for remuneration. But once licensed, RESA applies **even without** pay or when acting for yourself.
2. **Leasing** and **assignments** are trades in real estate. So are co-op, time share and shared interests, even if they're shares or contract rights.
3. "Real estate services" = **trading, rental PM, strata management**. Nothing else.
4. **Lawyers, executors, trustees, receivers and court-authorized people** are exempt under RESA. **Notaries, appraisers, inspectors, auctioneers, accountants (business sales) and developers' employees** are exempt under the **Regulation**.
5. Developer employees must disclose in a **separate** written document, **before** any agreement.
6. A licensee on **strata council** must disclose in writing, copy the managing broker, have **no sole signing authority**, and take **no pay**.
7. Unlicensed assistants can't **host open houses**, **show**, **advise**, **present/negotiate offers** or **talk to consumers** about deals. They **can** place signs, witness signatures and arrange rental repairs.
8. **Clients contract with the brokerage**, not the agent.
9. The **managing broker's category** limits what the whole brokerage can do.
10. If the **managing broker's licence goes inoperative**, so does the brokerage's and everyone else's.
11. **E&O**: **$2M** including defence costs; **$2,000** deductible per licensee; **claims-made**; **no deductible** if only defence costs; doesn't cover **penalties**, **own-interest deals**, **commission disputes**, **bodily injury**, **fraud**.
12. **Compensation Fund**: for **theft/fraud** by licensees; **$200,000** per claimant, **$1M** per brokerage; not for licensees' commission.
13. **REFBC** gets trust account **interest**, except client-specific accounts and tenants' deposits.
14. **realtor.ca is not the MLS®.**
15. **Board membership** isn't needed for a licence, but is needed for MLS® and the REALTOR® mark.
16. Minimum age **19**. Criminal record check within **90 days**. CELPIP **level 7**.
17. **Broker licences** need **2 years' experience in the past 5**.
18. Licence term **2 years**; renew **30+ days** before expiry.
19. Report **charges**, not just convictions.
20. Team members **can't split** buyer and seller sides of one deal.
21. A **PREC gives no liability shield.**

## Drill

**Q1.** A friend who owns a small office building asks you to find a tenant for a vacant suite. No money will change hands; "we're friends." Do RESA and your brokerage still matter?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**Yes.** Finding a tenant and negotiating a lease is **trading services** (a lease is a trade in real estate). Without pay, an unlicensed person wouldn't need a licence, but **once you're licensed, RESA applies to all your real estate services, paid or not**. You must do it **through your brokerage**, in its name, with proper records, like any other file.

</details>

**Q2.** Which of these people needs a licence to get paid for helping sell a property: (a) the executor of the owner's estate, (b) a notary, (c) a retired licensee whose licence lapsed, helping a neighbour sell for a $3,000 fee, (d) a developer's salaried employee selling that developer's new strata units?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) **Exempt** under RESA (executor, for estate property).
(b) **Exempt** under the Regulation (notaries).
(c) **Needs a licence.** No exemption applies; this is trading services for pay. Unlicensed activity can lead to BCFSA discipline and penalties of up to **$250,000**, and it's also an **offence** under RESA.
(d) **Exempt** under the Regulation, **if** they act only for their developer employer and give the required separate written disclosure before any agreement.

</details>

**Q3.** Your unlicensed assistant suggests: "I'll do the showings for your buyer this weekend, place the sign on your new listing, and witness the seller's signature on Monday." Which can they do?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**Placing the sign** and **witnessing signatures**: yes.
**Showings**: **no.** Showing property is trading services. (The only exception is a property manager's assistant showing rentals to prospective tenants.)

</details>

**Q4.** A buyer sues you and your brokerage for $600,000 over a negligent misrepresentation. Defence costs reach $250,000 and the court awards $450,000. How does the E&O Indemnity Plan respond, and what do you pay?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

The plan's **$2 million** limit **includes defence costs**, so total exposure ($250,000 + $450,000 = $700,000) is within the limit and covered, subject to the plan's terms. Because damages were paid, each insured pays a **$2,000 deductible**: **you $2,000, the brokerage $2,000**.

If the claim had been **dismissed**, there'd be **no deductible** (first-dollar defence).

</details>

**Q5.** A client tells you their previous agent pocketed their $40,000 deposit and disappeared. Which body can help, and what's the maximum?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

The **Real Estate Compensation Fund Corporation**, through the **Special Compensation Fund**, which covers money **misappropriated** or **fraudulently obtained** by a licensee. The client applies **in writing to BCFSA**, within the time limits. The maximum is **$200,000 per claimant** (and $1 million per brokerage), so $40,000 is within range.

E&O insurance **wouldn't** cover this: it excludes **dishonest or fraudulent** acts.

</details>

**Q6.** Your brokerage's only managing broker suddenly loses their licence. What happens to your licence, and why do most brokerages keep associate brokers?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

When the managing broker's licence becomes inoperative, suspended or cancelled, the **brokerage licence becomes inoperative**, and so do **all related licences**, including yours. You can't provide real estate services until it's fixed.

Brokerages keep **associate brokers** (already qualified as managing brokers) so one can step into the role quickly and the brokerage can be reinstated, which **automatically reinstates** everyone engaged there at that time.

</details>

**Q7 (applied).** Go back to the opening scenario. For each of the three offers, say what you'd do instead.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

1. **Cousin's lease ($2,000 cash):** take it on **through your brokerage**: a proper service agreement, records, and the fee paid **to the brokerage** (which then pays you). No cash on the side.
2. **Developer's $5,000/month:** you **can't be paid directly** by the developer or work for them outside your brokerage. If your brokerage takes on the project, you can work on it **through the brokerage**. And you'll need to follow **REDMA** (Lesson 5).
3. **Assistant hosting the open house:** **no.** Ask a **licensed colleague** at your brokerage to host, or reschedule.

</details>

## Mindset: your licence is a public trust

It's easy to see licensing rules as red tape between you and commissions. Try a different lens: **every rule in RESA is a scar from someone who got hurt.**

- The "everything through the brokerage" rule exists because side deals left clients with no records, no insurance, and no one to hold responsible.
- The unlicensed-assistant rules exist because clients thought they were getting advice from a professional and weren't.
- The Compensation Fund exists because some licensees stole deposits.
- The "report charges, not just convictions" rule exists because the public deserves to know about risks early.

When you see the harm behind each rule, the rules stop feeling arbitrary, and you'll spot grey areas the rules don't spell out.

> 🏢 **For commercial specifically.** Commercial clients are often sophisticated, and that tempts new licensees to cut corners: "they know what they're doing; they don't need the paperwork." The opposite is true. Bigger deals mean bigger claims, E&O limits run out faster, and the developer or investor offering you a side arrangement is testing whether you're a professional or a freelancer. Saying *"that has to go through my brokerage"* is how you show which one you are.

> **This week's habit.** Each time you're asked to do something in real estate, even casually, run the **three-question test**: *Is it a real estate service? Is it for someone else? Is anyone being paid?* Then add the licensee's fourth: *Does it go through my brokerage?*
