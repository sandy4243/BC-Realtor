## Why "other people's money" is where careers end

> **Scenario.** It's Friday at 6 p.m. Your buyer's offer on a $4.2M light-industrial building has just been accepted, and the buyer hands you a **$210,000 deposit cheque**. You think: "Completion isn't for 60 days, and the subjects aren't off yet. I'll drop it at the office on Monday, or maybe after the inspection, in case they back out."
>
> Over the weekend, the seller's agent calls. Their client wants to know the deposit is in trust. Meanwhile, the landlord of a strip mall you helped lease last year asks if you'd "just collect the rents for a few months" while they're travelling.
>
> And on Monday, a developer wants you to start taking deposits on a new industrial strata project next week. "The disclosure statement is nearly done."

Each of these is a trap covered in this lesson:

| Situation | The rule | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Holding the deposit until Monday or until subjects are removed | Money must go to the brokerage **promptly, meaning immediately** | Trust accounts |
| Collecting rent for the landlord | That's **rental property management**; a trading licence doesn't cover it | Property management by trading licensees |
| Taking pre-sale deposits before the disclosure statement is filed | **REDMA** forbids marketing until requirements are met | REDMA |
| Getting any of these wrong | Investigation, penalties up to **$250,000 per contravention**, suspension | Discipline |

Lesson 4 covered who can do what. This lesson covers **how you handle money and properties**, **what happens when licensees break the rules**, and **the special law for selling new developments**.

## Vocabulary first

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Trust account** | A bank account where the brokerage holds **other people's money** for a specific purpose. The brokerage can't use it for its own needs. |
| **Stakeholder** | A neutral holder of money that belongs to whichever party is entitled under the contract. Brokerages hold deposits as stakeholders, **not** as agent for either side. |
| **Misappropriation** | Using someone else's money for an unauthorized purpose. Theft, in plain terms. |
| **Professional misconduct** | Breaking RESA, its regulations or the Rules, or similar specific failures (see list below). |
| **Conduct unbecoming** | Conduct that harms the public interest, undermines confidence in, or disgraces the industry, even if no specific rule is broken. |
| **Undertaking** | A written promise to BCFSA to do (or stop doing) something, used to settle minor issues without formal discipline. |
| **Consent order** | A discipline order a licensee **agrees to** instead of going to a hearing. Published. |
| **Urgent order** | An immediate order (e.g. suspension) made **without a hearing** when waiting would harm the public. |
| **Administrative penalty** | A set fine for a specific listed contravention, **up to $100,000**, without a full discipline hearing. |
| **REDMA** | The *Real Estate Development Marketing Act*: rules for developers marketing multi-unit developments. |
| **Development unit** | A lot, strata lot, co-op interest, time share, shared interest or leasehold unit in a development covered by REDMA. |
| **Disclosure statement** | A developer's filed document disclosing all **material facts** about a development. |
| **Rescission** | Undoing a contract from the start, as if it never existed. |
| **Pre-sale** | Buying a unit before the development is finished (often before construction starts). |

## Property management by trading licensees

You'll get asked this constantly: a client buys an investment property and wants you to "look after it." What can a **trading-only** licensee do?

**The key point:** a "trade in real estate" includes **leasing**. So a trading licensee **can** help with the leasing transaction itself, but **not** with ongoing management.

| Service | Trading licence | Rental PM licence |
|---|---|---|
| Help a landlord **find tenants** (advertise, show, take applications) | ✅ | ✅ |
| Help a tenant **find a place** to rent | ✅ | ✅ |
| **Prepare** the lease, negotiate its terms, get the tenant's signature | ✅ | ✅ |
| Collect the tenant's **security deposit** | ✅ | ✅ |
| **Collect rent** for the owner | ❌ | ✅ |
| **Sign** the lease (or any contract) **on the owner's behalf** | ❌ | ✅ |
| Negotiate **other contracts** for the owner | ❌ | ✅ |
| **Make payments** to third parties for the owner | ❌ | ✅ |
| Do the **move-in inspection** | ❌ | ✅ |
| **Supervise** contractors or staff | ❌ | ✅ |
| **Manage** landlord–tenant matters | ❌ | ✅ |

**How to charge:** a trading licensee should charge a **one-time fee** for finding the tenant and completing the lease, and update the written service agreement to reflect it. **Never a monthly management fee**, since ongoing management isn't allowed.

> **Example.** Faizel, a trading licensee, helps Jasbir buy a rental condo, then advertises it and finds a tenant: **all fine**. Then Jasbir asks Faizel to collect the rent, organize repairs and handle the tenant. Faizel agrees to keep the relationship. **That's unlicensed rental property management.**
>
> What Faizel should have done: explain he isn't licensed for it, then **refer** Jasbir to a property manager in his brokerage or another brokerage. If he wants to offer it, he can get licensed by taking the **Rental Property Management** supplemental course.

> 🚨 **ALERT: this is one of the most common discipline issues.** Trading licensees doing rental management without the licence have been fined **$3,500 to $7,500** plus **$1,500** costs each, often with a remedial course.

**Teams:** a licensee with both trading and rental PM licences who's on a team can do property management **only outside the team**.

### Managing your own (or family's) rentals: Rules ss.94–95

You **can** manage rental property owned by **yourself**, your **spouse**, **children** or **parents** outside your brokerage, if you:

- act in **your own name**, not the brokerage's;
- tell each prospective tenant, **before** the tenancy agreement, that although you're licensed you're **not regulated under RESA** for this;
- **disclose in writing** to the family member that you're not acting as a licensee and aren't regulated under RESA for this;
- **disclose in writing** to your managing broker that you're doing it; and
- do it **for free**.

⚠️ Doesn't cover **siblings, cousins, aunts, uncles** or **unrelated investors**.

**Back to the scenario:** collecting the strip mall's rents is **rental property management**. Unless you hold that licence, refer it.

## Trust accounts: other people's money

### The basics (RESA s.26 onward)

- Every brokerage must keep **one or more interest-bearing trust accounts** at a savings institution **in BC**.
- Money held for a **principal** (the client or party whose money it is) goes there, and must only be used for its intended purpose. **Never** for office bills or paying commissions early.
- A licensee must **promptly** give the brokerage **all money** received for or from a principal, or as remuneration. The brokerage must **promptly** deposit it in trust.
- **Exception:** cheques, drafts or money orders payable to **third parties** (not the brokerage) can just be **delivered** to the payee.

> 🚨 **"Promptly" means immediately.** BCFSA auditors often find deposit cheques held until an offer is accepted or subjects are removed. **That's not allowed.** Don't hold money waiting for some future event.

**Back to the scenario:** the $210,000 cheque goes to your brokerage **right away**, not Monday "or after the inspection."

### Who gets the interest?

| Account | Interest goes to |
|---|---|
| **General** brokerage trust account | The **Real Estate Foundation of BC** |
| **Separate** account set up **at a client's request** | **Whoever the client chooses** |
| Separate account for **tenants' deposits** (where the RTA requires interest) | The **tenant** |

> 💡 On a large commercial deposit held for months, the interest can be significant. A client can ask for a **separate** trust account so they (not the Foundation) get it. Worth mentioning to commercial clients.

### When can money come out?

A brokerage can withdraw trust money only if it is:

1. paid in **by mistake**;
2. **interest** going to the Foundation;
3. **remuneration** for a licensee that has been **earned** under the Rules (for a sale: when the transfer documents are **submitted for registration**; Lesson 3);
4. **unclaimed money** transferred under RESA;
5. **paid into court** because of a dispute over who's entitled;
6. paid under a **court order**; or
7. paid to, or as directed by, the **principal** it belongs to.

### Deposits on a sale: the stakeholder rule (RESA s.28)

Once buyer and seller have an agreement, the brokerage holds the deposit as a **stakeholder**, **not as agent for either party**, even if the brokerage acts for one of them. So the deposit can come out only if:

- one of items 1–6 above applies, **or**
- **both parties agree in writing**, **or**
- the buyer exercises the **Home Buyer Rescission Period** (Lesson 8): the brokerage pays the seller the rescission fee from the deposit and returns the rest to the buyer.

> 🔎 **Why stakeholder?** If the deal falls apart, the listing brokerage might be tempted to side with its own client. The stakeholder rule stops either side grabbing the money; it waits for agreement or a court.

### Two modern risks

> ⚠️ **Cryptocurrency.** Crypto **can't be held in a brokerage trust account**. A crypto deposit must be held by a third party, **outside** RESA's protections, where it can be misappropriated, and its value can swing wildly. Know your brokerage's policy, warn the client of the risks, **strongly recommend legal advice**, and **document** that you did.

> 🚨 **ALERT: money laundering through trust accounts.** A criminal makes an offer with a large deposit, lets the offer fail, and asks for the deposit back. The money now comes from a **brokerage trust account** and looks clean. Watch for odd deposit patterns and speak to your managing broker if anything seems off. (More in Lesson 6.)

## A map of the Rules

The **Real Estate Services Rules** are the day-to-day rulebook. **Most discipline cases are about breaking a Rule**, often one the licensee had never read. You'll meet specific Rules throughout the course; for now, know where things live:

| Part | Covers |
|---|---|
| **1. General** | **Definitions**. Always check whether a word in a Rule has a special meaning. |
| **2. Licensing** | Licence categories, brokerage offices, qualifications, licence terms, communications with BCFSA, discipline and enforcement (including administrative penalties, s.26). |
| **3. General responsibilities** | Managing broker duties (**s.28**), associate broker and representative duties (**s.29**), **duties to clients (s.30)**: act in their best interests, avoid conflicts, act within authority. **Honesty (s.33)**. **Reasonable care and skill (s.34)**. |
| **4. Business practices** | General practices, licensee names, **advertising**. |
| **5. Relationships with principals and parties** | Service agreements, **disclosures**, remuneration, dual agency and conflicts. |
| **6. Dealing with others** | No paying **unlicensed** people for licensed work; no acting for unlicensed people unless they're a principal. |
| **7. Accounts and finances** | **Trust accounting**, account monitoring, financial statements, activity reports. |
| **8. Records** | Financial and other records; record keeping; **records BCFSA can demand** (ad hoc data calls, s.93.1; event-triggered filings, s.93.2). |
| **9. Licensee exemptions** | Managing your own or family's rentals (ss.94–95), sitting on strata council (s.96). |

> 🧠 **Four section numbers worth knowing:** **s.30** (duties to clients), **s.33** (honesty), **s.34** (reasonable care and skill), and **s.29** (your duties as a representative, including reporting misconduct). They appear in disciplinary decisions all the time.

## Discipline and enforcement

### What counts as misconduct (RESA s.35)

**Professional misconduct** includes:

- breaching **RESA**, the **Regulation**, the **Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation** or the **Rules**;
- breaching a **condition** on your licence;
- **wrongful taking** or **deceptive dealing**;
- **incompetence** in licensed activities;
- failing to **cooperate with an investigation** or **comply with an order**;
- breaking an **undertaking**; and
- making or allowing a **false or misleading statement** in a RESA document.

**Conduct unbecoming a licensee** is conduct that:

- is **contrary to the public's best interests**,
- **undermines public confidence** in the industry, or
- **brings the industry into disrepute**.

**Anyone**, licensee or not, can complain to BCFSA.

### From complaint to outcome

```
Complaint or BCFSA's own concern
   │
   ├─▶ Undertaking (minor/obvious issue; not public)
   │
   ├─▶ Administrative penalty (listed contraventions; up to $100,000)
   │
   └─▶ Investigation
          │
          └─▶ Notice of discipline hearing (≥ 21 days' notice)
                 │
                 ├─▶ Consent order (licensee agrees; no hearing)
                 └─▶ Hearing → dismissed, or sanctions
                        │
                        └─▶ Appeal to Financial Services Tribunal (within 30 days)

Urgent order: possible at any point, without notice or hearing, if delay would harm the public.
```

### Undertakings (RESA s.53.1)

For **minor or obvious** misconduct, BCFSA can, with or without an investigation, explain its concern and accept a **written undertaking** to meet specific terms (possibly including paying BCFSA's costs).

- **Not part of your public record** and generally **not published**.
- **Binding.** Breaking an undertaking **is** professional misconduct.

### Investigations (RESA ss.37–38)

BCFSA can investigate **with or without a complaint**: current or former licensees, and officers, directors, controlling shareholders or partners of a licensee.

- It can **inspect, copy and remove records** from business premises (during business hours), require you to **answer questions** and **produce records**.
- You **can't withhold, hide or destroy** information.
- With a **justice's order**, it can **seize** records or evidence wherever they are.

> ⚠️ **Rules s.21:** respond to BCFSA **promptly**, **in writing** (unless told otherwise) and **within the deadline**. Ignoring BCFSA is misconduct in itself.

### The discipline hearing (RESA ss.40–42)

- BCFSA issues a **notice of discipline hearing** at least **21 days** ahead, describing the allegations, the time and place, and that BCFSA **can proceed without you**.
- You can propose a **consent order**: agree to a specific order and skip the hearing. (Your proposal can be used for a Special Compensation Fund claim, but **not** in civil lawsuits.)
- Otherwise there's a hearing, written, oral or both, possibly after a **pre-hearing conference**. BCFSA's lawyers present evidence; you can have a lawyer or represent yourself; witnesses testify **under oath**; a court reporter records it.
- BCFSA either **dismisses** the matter or finds misconduct and imposes **sanctions**.

### Urgent orders (RESA s.45)

If waiting for an investigation or hearing would harm the public, BCFSA can **immediately suspend** a licence, impose conditions, or order someone to stop or do something, **without notice and without a hearing**. The licensee gets the order, written reasons, and notice that they can **demand a hearing**. After the hearing, BCFSA either **rescinds** the order or replaces it with a disciplinary order.

> **Case (2017).** A brokerage ran a **"rent to own" scheme** aimed at homeowners facing **foreclosure**. Sellers were pushed to sell **well below assessed value**, given confusing buy-back options at much higher prices, and leased back their homes at **above-market rents**, with any tiny lease default cancelling the option. None had their own representation. Preparing a full hearing would take a year, and the conduct would continue, so licences were **suspended immediately**.

### Sanctions (RESA s.43)

If misconduct is found, BCFSA must do **one or more** of:

1. **Reprimand**
2. **Suspend** the licence
3. **Cancel** the licence
4. Impose **restrictions or conditions**
5. Order the licensee to **stop or carry out** an activity
6. Require a **course** of study or training
7. **Prohibit** applying for a licence for a period
8. Require payment of BCFSA's **enforcement expenses**
9. A **discipline penalty** of up to **$500,000** (brokerage) or **$250,000** (anyone else), **per contravention**
10. A **return of compensation penalty**: up to the **remuneration** earned from the misconduct

> 💡 **Per contravention** matters. Failing to give the required disclosure form to **three** clients = **three** contraventions, each up to $250,000.

BCFSA can also **freeze funds** and order property held in trust for a receiver (usually alongside an urgent order), and apply to court to appoint a **receiver** to wind up or manage the licensee's business (s.59).

**Money from penalties** can only be spent on **educating** the public and industry about the industry's regulation and real estate issues.

**Everything's public.** BCFSA must publish consent orders, discipline orders and urgent orders (s.47). They're linked to the licensee in **BCFSA's online licensee search**, and on **CanLII**. Your clients can look you up.

### Unlicensed persons

BCFSA can investigate and discipline **unlicensed** people (and licensees acting outside their licence category) with similar powers: investigations, seizure orders, hearings, consent and urgent orders, freezes. It can order them to **stop**, **remedy** the situation, pay **costs**, pay a penalty of up to **$500,000** (corporations and partnerships) or **$250,000** (individuals), plus **return of remuneration**.

### Appeals: Financial Services Tribunal

- Most BCFSA decisions can be appealed to the **Financial Services Tribunal** (RESA s.54).
- **In writing** (unless the Tribunal allows otherwise), filed within **30 days** of the decision.
- **An appeal doesn't pause the order.** A suspended licence stays suspended unless and until the Tribunal reverses it.

### Ten things to know about complaints

1. **Complaints and investigations are confidential**, except as needed to investigate.
2. **You'll be contacted** and given a chance to respond.
3. **Resolve misunderstandings early**, before they become complaints.
4. Files can be **closed at any stage** for lack of evidence.
5. If a hearing is needed, you can **contest** it or **agree to a consent order**.
6. **Consent orders and hearing decisions are published.**
7. **Hearings are public**, unless held privately to protect confidential information.
8. **You'll be kept informed** of investigations and hearings.
9. You can **attend**, have a **lawyer**, and may be asked to **give evidence**.
10. Information that would be an **unreasonable invasion of privacy** isn't published.

### Administrative penalties

A faster, proportionate tool for listed contraventions: a step between a **warning letter** and a **full discipline hearing**.

- Up to **$100,000** per contravention, possibly with required courses or licence conditions.
- **Six categories** (Rules s.26):

| Category | Covers | |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Business management infractions | Low risk to consumers |
| **B** | Minor matters, no real harm | Where a penalty serves the public interest better than dismissal or a hearing |
| **C** | Substantial **duties to clients and others** | Higher risk; **higher penalties** |
| **D** | Minor, but timely compliance matters | **Applied until you comply** |
| **E** | **Failing to cooperate with investigations** | **Applied until you comply** |
| **F** | **Unlicensed activity** | **Highest** penalties, set as ranges |

- Process: BCFSA sends a **notice of administrative penalty**. You have **30 days** (or longer if allowed) to **pay** or **ask to be heard**. BCFSA then **confirms or cancels** it. Decisions are generally **published**.

### Offences (RESA ss.118–119)

Some conduct is a **provincial offence**, prosecuted in court, not just a discipline matter:

- providing services **without a licence**,
- **failing to pay money into trust**,
- **improperly withdrawing** trust money,
- **interfering with an investigation**,
- **failing to comply** with a BCFSA order, and
- making a **false statement** in a required record.

| | First conviction | Each later conviction |
|---|---|---|
| **Corporation** | Up to **$1.25 million** | Up to **$2.5 million** |
| **Individual** | Up to **$1.25 million** and/or **2 years' jail** | Up to **$2.5 million** and/or **2 years' jail** |

> 🧠 **Keep the dollar figures straight:**
> - **$100,000**: administrative penalty (max per contravention)
> - **$250,000 / $500,000**: discipline penalty (individual / brokerage), per contravention
> - **$1.25M / $2.5M + 2 years**: court fine for an offence (first / subsequent)
> - **$200,000 / $1M**: Compensation Fund maximum (per claimant / per brokerage)
> - **$2M**: E&O limit

## REDMA: selling new developments

### What it is

The **Real Estate Development Marketing Act** regulates **developers** marketing **development units** in a **development property**. It applies to developments **anywhere**, if they're marketed **in BC**.

Before marketing, a developer must:

1. meet the **preliminary approvals** for that type of development;
2. arrange for **utilities and services** to be paid for, and secure the purchaser's **title** or interest; and
3. **file a disclosure statement** with BCFSA, in BCFSA's required form.

A **developer** is anyone who owns, leases or has the right to acquire or dispose of development property (with some exclusions). Licensees must market under the **developer's name**, not another entity's.

### What's a "development property"?

| Type | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Subdivision lots** | **5 or more** (unless every lot is **64.7 ha** or more) |
| **Bare land strata lots** | **5 or more** |
| **Strata lots** in a building | **5 or more** |
| **Cooperative interests** | **2 or more** |
| **Time share interests** | **5 or more** |
| **Shared interests in land** | **2 or more** |
| **Leasehold units** in a residential leasehold complex | **5 or more** |

> 🧠 **Memory trick: "Five, except co-ops and shares, which are two."**

**Exemptions** (from the REDMA Regulation) include:

- selling the **whole development in one transaction** (developer to developer);
- units **zoned and used for industrial or commercial** purposes;
- **leases of 3 years or less**;
- sales already complying with the **Securities Act**; and
- **subdivision lots in a municipality** where the developer has met the municipality's subdivision bylaws or posted the required security.

> 🏢 **Commercial angle.** The **industrial/commercial exemption** matters to you. A new industrial strata or office condo project sold to businesses may be **exempt** from REDMA's disclosure statement requirement. But don't assume. Mixed-use projects with residential units aren't exempt for those units, and the exemption has conditions. Confirm it, ideally with the developer's lawyer.

### Types of development units

- **Subdivision lot**: land divided other than under the *Strata Property Act*.
- **Strata lot** (building or bare land): can't be marketed until **either** the strata plan is deposited **or** a **building permit** (or approving officer's preliminary approval) is issued. Early marketing is allowed in some cases under BCFSA Policy Statements 5 and 6.
- **Cooperative interest**: a share or membership in a company or partnership **plus a right to occupy** land. Exempt from Securities Act registration, so **licensees can sell them**. *Example: a company owns a fourplex and sells one share with each unit's occupancy right.*
- **Time share interest**: a right to **recurring periodic use** (traditionally a week a year; now also **fractional ownership**, e.g. 1/5 of a property for about 10 weeks a year).
- **Shared interest in land**: buying land **with others** plus an agreement limiting you to **part** of it. *Example: eight buyers each get 1/8 as tenants in common, each with a contract to occupy one apartment; no strata plan.*
- **Leasehold unit** in a residential leasehold complex, including **life leases** often sold to seniors.

### Your duties when acting for a developer

1. **Verify a disclosure statement has been filed** with BCFSA before offering any development unit.
2. **Give the buyer the disclosure statement** (and pre-sale summary form, if required) **before** they sign, and get their **written acknowledgement**.
3. **Check that the deposit terms** in the purchase agreement match the disclosure statement. If not, **recommend legal advice**.
4. Remember REDMA covers **out-of-province** properties marketed in BC.

### Pre-marketing

Before the disclosure statement is filed, a developer **can** advertise and talk to potential buyers, as long as no one gets the impression they have a **right to buy** a unit. BCFSA recommends every ad include:

- the developer's **name and address**,
- a **contact** for information and the disclosure statement (when available), and
- a **prominent disclaimer** that it's **not an offering for sale**, and that an offering can only be made after a disclosure statement is filed.

**Back to the scenario:** "the disclosure statement is nearly done" isn't enough. **No deposits, no agreements** until it's filed (unless an exemption clearly applies).

### Pre-sales: what to tell buyer clients

Pre-sales can be good business, but buyers need to understand the risks:

- **Delays or cancellation**: poor sales, financing or permit delays, rising costs, labour shortages.
- **Value and financing changes**: by completion, prices may fall, rates rise or the buyer's job change, so the lender may lend less than expected.
- **Assignment limits**: many contracts restrict assigning to a new buyer, or charge an **assignment fee**. Recommend legal advice.
- **Finished product**: developers can usually **substitute materials and finishes**.

BCFSA's **"Understanding Pre-Sale Purchases"** guide is a good handout.

### Disclosure statements

- Must be in BCFSA's form and **plainly disclose all material facts**, **without misrepresentation** (s.14(2)).
- A developer **can't sign** a purchase agreement until the buyer has **received** the disclosure statement, had a **chance to read it**, and **acknowledged that in writing** (s.15(1)).
- Buyers are **deemed to have relied** on it, so they can sue for **damages** for a **material** misrepresentation (s.22(3)), subject to defences.

**Summary of Pre-sale Risks and Buyer Rights form** (Policy Statement 14): for developments **not yet complete** (plan not deposited, or promised buildings or services not finished), this form goes **in front of the cover page**. It highlights key provisions, points buyers to important sections, and urges professional advice. The developer's licensees **explain it and get the buyer's initials**; buyer's agents should check it's attached and be able to explain it.

> ⚠️ **The developer's disclosure doesn't replace yours.** You still have your own duties, including disclosing **material latent defects** you know about (Rules s.59).

### Material changes: amendment or new statement?

When the developer learns the statement is wrong, misleading or non-compliant, it must file and deliver an **amendment** or a **new** disclosure statement to prospective buyers and to buyers who haven't yet got title. (A **consolidated** statement can combine the original and all amendments.)

A **new** statement is required when:

- the **developer's identity changes**,
- a **receiver, liquidator or trustee in bankruptcy** is appointed over the property, or
- **BCFSA requires** one.

**Why it matters:** a **new** disclosure statement gives buyers a **fresh 7-day rescission right**. An **amendment** doesn't.

### Rescission rights under REDMA

A buyer can **rescind** (walk away) by **written notice to the developer**. These rights **can't be waived**; any clause trying to waive them is void. Don't advise on the process; **refer the buyer to a lawyer**.

| Right | When |
|---|---|
| **1. Seven-day right** | Within **7 days** after the **later** of: signing the agreement, or the developer getting the buyer's **written acknowledgement** of the disclosure statement. **For any reason.** |
| **2. No disclosure statement delivered** | **Any time.** The agreement is also generally **unenforceable** against the buyer (s.23). |
| **3. Amendment not delivered** | If the amendment was **reasonably relevant** to the buyer's decision, and **no more than one year** has passed since **title transferred**. |

> ⚠️ **Use a stand-alone receipt.** A clause buried in the purchase agreement saying the buyer has read the disclosure statement isn't enough. BCFSA recommends a **separate** written acknowledgement.

**Don't mix this up with the Home Buyer Rescission Period (HBRP).** The HBRP, under the *Property Law Act*, lets a buyer of **residential real property** rescind within **3 business days** by paying **0.25%** of the price. It **doesn't apply** to REDMA purchases, which have their own rights.

### Penalties under REDMA

- **Offence** for failing to meet disclosure requirements: same scale as RESA (**$1.25M / $2.5M**, up to **2 years** for individuals).
- BCFSA can order a developer to **stop marketing**, or impose an **administrative penalty** of up to **$500,000** (corporation) or **$250,000** (individual).

Licensees have been **disciplined** for:

1. taking pay **directly from a developer** instead of through the brokerage;
2. helping a developer handle **deposits** contrary to REDMA;
3. not recommending **legal advice** where deposit terms broke REDMA;
4. marketing **out-of-province** units in BC with **no filed disclosure statement**; and
5. preparing a purchase agreement containing a **waiver of rescission rights**.

## Two alerts

> 🚨 **ALERT 1: Trust money moves today, not later.** Every deposit goes to the brokerage and into trust immediately. Not after acceptance, not after subjects, not Monday.

> 🚨 **ALERT 2: No disclosure statement, no deal.** If REDMA applies, nothing gets offered, signed or deposited until the disclosure statement is filed and in the buyer's hands, with a separate written receipt.

## Trap list: things the exam likes to twist

1. Trading licensees **can** find tenants, prepare and negotiate the lease, and collect the **security deposit**. They **can't** collect **rent**, **sign for the owner**, do the **move-in inspection**, **supervise contractors** or **manage** tenancies.
2. Trading licensees charge a **one-time** leasing fee, **never** a monthly management fee.
3. Managing family rentals: **spouse, children, parents** only; not siblings or cousins.
4. "**Promptly**" means **immediately**.
5. Trust interest → **REFBC**, except **client-requested separate accounts** and **tenants' deposits**.
6. On a sale, the brokerage holds the deposit as a **stakeholder**, **not** as agent for its own client.
7. **Crypto can't be held in trust.**
8. **Professional misconduct** = breaking specific rules; **conduct unbecoming** = harming public confidence or interest.
9. **Undertakings aren't published**, but breaking one is misconduct.
10. Notice of discipline hearing: **at least 21 days**.
11. **Urgent orders**: no notice, no hearing; licensee can demand a hearing afterwards.
12. Discipline penalty caps are **per contravention**.
13. Appeal to the **Financial Services Tribunal** within **30 days**; the order **stays in force** during the appeal.
14. **Administrative penalties**: up to **$100,000**; **30 days** to pay or ask to be heard; categories **D and E** apply **until compliance**; **F** = unlicensed activity, highest.
15. Offences: **$1.25M** first, **$2.5M** after, up to **2 years** jail for individuals.
16. REDMA thresholds: **5** for most; **2** for **co-op interests** and **shared interests**. Subdivision lots of **64.7 ha+** don't count.
17. **Industrial/commercial** units are **exempt** from REDMA's marketing requirements.
18. Strata lots can be marketed once the **plan is deposited or a building permit is issued**.
19. **New** disclosure statement → **new 7-day right**. **Amendment** → no new right.
20. **7-day right** runs from the **later** of agreement or written acknowledgement.
21. No disclosure statement → rescind **any time**. Missed amendment → within **1 year after title transfer**, if reasonably relevant.
22. **REDMA rescission rights can't be waived.**
23. The **HBRP** (3 business days, 0.25%) **doesn't apply** to REDMA sales.

## Drill

**Q1.** At 9 p.m. on a Saturday, your buyer gives you a deposit cheque payable to your brokerage "in trust". The offer is still being negotiated. What do you do with it, and when?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

Deliver it to your brokerage **promptly, meaning immediately** (the next opportunity, not after the offer is accepted or subjects removed), so the brokerage can deposit it in trust. Holding it pending a future event is exactly what BCFSA auditors flag. If the offer isn't accepted, the brokerage returns it according to the rules.

</details>

**Q2.** A commercial deal collapses. The seller says the buyer breached and wants the $300,000 deposit; the buyer says the condition wasn't satisfied and wants it back. Your brokerage acted for the seller. Can your brokerage just release it to the seller?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**No.** Once there's an agreement, the brokerage holds the deposit as a **stakeholder**, not as the seller's agent. It can release it only with **both parties' written agreement**, under a **court order**, by **paying it into court** because of the dispute, or under one of the other narrow statutory grounds. Both parties should get legal advice.

</details>

**Q3.** Your investor client asks you (trading licence only) to: (a) advertise their new retail unit for lease, (b) negotiate the lease with a tenant, (c) sign the lease on their behalf, (d) collect the first month's rent and the security deposit, (e) arrange the HVAC contractor. Which can you do?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) **Yes**: finding a tenant is trading services.
(b) **Yes**: negotiating the lease terms is trading services.
(c) **No**: signing contracts **on the owner's behalf** is rental property management.
(d) **Security deposit: yes. Rent: no.** Collecting rent is rental property management.
(e) **No**: supervising or arranging contractors is management.

Charge a **one-time** leasing fee, update the service agreement, and refer the management to a licensed property manager.

</details>

**Q4.** A licensee is found to have failed to give the required disclosure to four clients. What's the maximum discipline penalty, and could BCFSA also take the commission?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

Penalties apply **per contravention**: up to **$250,000 × 4 = $1,000,000** for an individual. BCFSA can also impose a **return of compensation penalty** up to the remuneration earned from the misconduct, plus other sanctions (suspension, courses, enforcement expenses). The decision will be **published** and linked to the licensee's public record.

</details>

**Q5.** BCFSA suspends your licence. You file an appeal with the Financial Services Tribunal the next week. Can you keep working while you wait?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**No.** BCFSA's order **stays in effect** during the appeal unless the Tribunal reverses it. The appeal must be **written** and filed within **30 days** of the decision.

</details>

**Q6.** A developer is selling 12 strata units in a new residential tower. A buyer signs on March 1 and gives the developer a written acknowledgement of receiving the disclosure statement on March 4. On March 10, the buyer changes their mind. Can they rescind?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**Yes.** The 7-day right runs from the **later** of the agreement (March 1) and the written acknowledgement (March 4), so it lasts until **March 11**. On March 10 they can rescind **for any reason** by **written notice to the developer**. Refer them to a lawyer for the mechanics. The **HBRP** doesn't apply to REDMA purchases.

</details>

**Q7 (applied).** A developer client is building a 20-unit **industrial strata** project and wants you to start taking deposits next week. The disclosure statement "isn't quite done." What do you check?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

1. **Does REDMA apply?** Units **zoned and used for industrial or commercial purposes** are exempt from REDMA's marketing requirements. Confirm the zoning and intended use, check there are **no residential units** (e.g. caretaker suites or live-work), and get confirmation from the developer's lawyer.
2. **If REDMA applies:** no marketing, offers or deposits until the **disclosure statement is filed** with BCFSA and the **strata plan is deposited or a building permit issued**. Pre-marketing only with the recommended disclaimers.
3. **Deposits** go into **trust** through your brokerage and must match whatever the disclosure statement says.
4. **Your pay comes through your brokerage**, never directly from the developer.
5. Whether or not REDMA applies, your own **disclosure duties** (like known material latent defects) still apply.

</details>

## Mindset: protect the money, protect the career

The fastest way to end a real estate career isn't losing a deal. It's **mishandling money** or **stepping outside your licence**. Those are the issues BCFSA treats most seriously, the ones E&O insurance doesn't cover, and the ones that end up **published under your name**.

The good news: they're also the **easiest to avoid**. They don't take talent. They take **habits**:

- Every dollar goes straight to the brokerage.
- Every service matches your licence.
- Every developer deal starts with *"Where's the filed disclosure statement?"*
- Every BCFSA letter gets a prompt, written reply.

> 🏢 **For commercial specifically.** Commercial deposits are large, timelines are long, and clients are busy. That's exactly when shortcuts look tempting: holding a cheque "until the deal firms up", helping a landlord client "temporarily" with rent, taking a developer's word that paperwork is coming. The professionals who last in commercial are the ones whose clients know **their money is always exactly where it should be**.

> **This week's habit.** Look up a few licensees (including yourself, once licensed) on **BCFSA's public licensee search**, and read one or two recent **discipline decisions** on BCFSA's site or CanLII. For each, ask: *"What one habit would have prevented this?"* You'll find the answer is almost always simple.
