## Why agency is the heart of your job

> **Scenario.** You're the designated agent for the seller of a $4.5M strip mall.
>
> - At an open house, a buyer with no agent says, "I'll offer $4.3M. What do you think the seller will really take?"
> - The next day, **another of your clients**, an investor you've been helping for months, says, "That strip mall looks perfect. Write me an offer."
> - A mortgage broker offers you **$1,000** for every client you send them.
>
> Each of these is an **agency** question. Answering the first buyer could create an **implied agency** (and illegal **dual agency**). Your investor client and your seller now have **conflicting interests**. The referral fee must be **disclosed**. Get these wrong and you face lawsuits, lost commission and discipline.

Everything a licensee does rests on one idea: you act **on behalf of** someone else. This lesson covers how that relationship is created, what you owe your client (and what they owe you), how BC's **designated agency** system works, why **dual agency** is banned, and the **disclosure forms** you must use.

## Vocabulary first

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Agency** | A relationship where one person (the **agent**) is authorized to represent and act for another (the **principal**) in dealing with others. |
| **Principal / client** | The person the agent works for. |
| **Third party** | Someone the agent deals with on the principal's behalf (e.g. the buyer, when you act for the seller). |
| **Warranty of authority** | The agent's implied promise to third parties that they really have the authority they're using. |
| **Fiduciary** | Someone in a position of trust who must act **solely** in the other person's interests. |
| **Designated agency** | BC's model since 2012: the brokerage names specific licensees as the client's **sole** agents; the rest of the brokerage isn't the client's agent. |
| **Brokerage agency** | Older model: the **whole brokerage** (every licensee) is the client's agent. |
| **Dual agency** | One brokerage/licensee representing clients with **conflicting** interests in the same trade. **Banned** in BC since 2018, with one narrow exception. |
| **Unrepresented party** | A consumer a licensee helps in a limited way **without** being their agent ("no agency"). |
| **Express / implied / apparent authority** | Authority given in words / authority needed to carry out what was given / authority a third party reasonably believes exists. |
| **Estoppel** | A rule stopping someone denying a situation they led others to believe in. |
| **Ratification** | The principal approving, after the fact, something the agent did without authority. |
| **Secret profit** | Any undisclosed benefit an agent gets from acting for the principal. |
| **Net listing** | Commission = whatever the sale price exceeds a set figure. **Prohibited** (Rules s.60). |
| **Double-ending** | The listing brokerage keeping the whole commission because there's no cooperating brokerage. |
| **DORTS** | The **Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services** form (Rules s.54). |

## The agency relationship

People hire agents because the agent has **skills** the principal lacks, can be **somewhere** the principal can't, or saves the principal **time**.

- The **agent** can usually **bind** the principal to contracts with third parties. The contract is between the **principal and the third party**; the agent isn't a party.
- When dealing with third parties, the agent gives a **warranty of authority**: an implied promise that they're acting within their authority.

> 💡 **Example.** Chan hires Stella, an interior designer, to furnish his home for up to $50,000. Stella signs a painting contract "as agent for Chan". **Chan** must pay the painters. If Stella signed without saying she was an agent, she could be **personally** liable.

**In BC real estate, your authority is narrower.** A typical listing or buyer agency agreement lets you market, show, negotiate and present offers, but **not sign contracts** for the client. That needs separate **written** authority (Rules **s.45**, below).

> 🔑 **The big three fiduciary duties** (more later): **loyalty** (client first, ahead of everyone, including you), **confidentiality**, and **avoiding conflicts of interest**. Keep them in mind through the whole lesson.

## How real estate agency evolved in BC

| Era | Model | The problem |
|---|---|---|
| **Before 1994** | **Seller sub-agency**: the licensee working with the buyer (the "selling" or "cooperating" agent) was legally a **sub-agent of the seller** | Buyers assumed "their" agent worked for them. They didn't; all duties ran to the seller. |
| **1994** | **Assumed buyer agency**: the selling agent is presumed to be the **buyer's** agent | Still **brokerage agency**: every licensee in the brokerage was the client's agent, so two licensees in one office on opposite sides put the brokerage in conflict. Fixed with **limited dual agency** agreements that nobody really understood. |
| **June 2012** | **Designated agency**: specific licensees are the client's **sole** agents | In-house deals no longer a conflict, but some licensees still used limited dual agency for **their own** buyer and seller. |
| **2018** | **Dual agency banned** (Rules), one narrow exception | Current system. |

**Commission splits survived every change.** The seller's brokerage still usually shares commission with the buyer's brokerage, so many BC buyers expect never to pay their own agent.

### Designated agency today

The client signs with the **brokerage**, but the brokerage designates one or more licensees as the client's **sole agents**. Only they owe the full agency duties. (Rules **ss.31–32** allow the client and brokerage to agree in writing that the s.30 duties are owed by the designated agent(s) only.)

Under **Rules s.32**, a brokerage offering designated agency must:

1. **supervise** its designated agents
2. keep each client's **confidential information** confidential (unless authorized or required by law)
3. treat all clients' interests **even-handedly, objectively and impartially**

**Brokerage agency** is still the **only** model allowed for brokerages that do **only** rental and/or strata management.

## The ban on dual agency

Since **2018**, the Rules prohibit **dual agency**: a brokerage representing clients with **conflicting interests** in the same trade. Conflicting parties include:

- a **buyer** and a **seller**
- a **lessor** and a **lessee**
- an **assignor** and an **assignee**
- **two or more buyers** (or lessees, or assignees) interested in the **same property**

It **isn't** dual agency when **different designated agents** in the same brokerage act for each side.

**Why?** The **Independent Advisory Group** (2016 report) found a dual agent can't be fully **loyal** to two clients with opposite goals, can't advise without leaking **confidential** information, and may be tempted by the **whole commission**.

> 🧭 **The unrepresented buyer at your open house.** Atticus is the designated agent for seller Jane. Ravi, who has no agent, asks Atticus to represent him. Atticus **can't**. Ravi's options:
>
> 1. **Same brokerage, different designated agent**: ABC Brokerage assigns another licensee to Ravi.
> 2. **No agency**: Ravi stays unrepresented and Atticus gives **limited** help (preparing and presenting offers at Ravi's direction, explaining terms and forms, estimating costs), with the **DORTS** and **Disclosure of Risks to Unrepresented Parties** forms.
> 3. **Different brokerage** entirely.

### The narrow exception (Rules s.64)

Dual agency is allowed **only** if the property is in a **remote** location that's **under-served** by licensees **and** it's **impracticable** for different licensees to act for each party.

- "**Impracticable**" means **not capable of being done**, not just **inconvenient**. BCFSA reads "remote" and "under-served" **strictly**; it's rare.
- Before any services: give each party the **Disclosure of Risks Associated with Dual Agency** form, **signed by the managing broker**, who must explain how **each** of the three elements is met.
- Then sign a **written dual agency agreement** with each party (both must consent to equal treatment instead of loyalty).
- The brokerage sends copies of the disclosure forms to **BCFSA**.
- Talk to your managing broker first; get legal advice if unsure.

## Standard forms: prescribed vs other

| Category | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **BCFSA prescribed forms** | **Must** use the exact form; **can't alter** it | Disclosure of Interest in Trade (s.53), **DORTS** (s.54), Disclosure of Risks to Unrepresented Parties (s.55), Disclosure of Risks Associated with Dual Agency (s.64), Disclosure to Sellers of Expected Remuneration (s.57), Notice to Seller Regarding Assignment Terms (Reg s.8.2) |
| **BCFSA non-mandatory forms** | Any form with the required content works | Disclosure of Remuneration – Trading Services (s.56) |
| **BCREA standard forms** | Created by BCREA's Standard Forms Committee for REALTORS®; mandatory only if an organization's own rules say so | **Multiple Listing Contract**, **Buyer's Agency Exclusive Contract**, **Buyer Agency Acknowledgement**, Contract of Purchase and Sale |

The BCREA agency forms are what **create designated agency** in practice. Some clients (e.g. big developers) insist on **their own** forms. That's fine as long as the regulatory requirements are met.

## Who can be an agent?

**Common law:** anyone of **sound mind**, even a **minor**, because the agent isn't a party to the principal's contract.

> 🔎 ***Travelers Guarantee v. Farajollahi* (2012 BCSC).** A father, overseas, asked his **14-year-old** daughter by phone to sign a loan contract for him. **He was bound.** But a minor acting under their **own** agency contract can rely on their incapacity to cancel it.

**RESA** adds: to provide real estate services for pay, you must be **licensed** (education, **19+**, good reputation) or exempt.

## Creating an agency relationship

### 1. Express contract (the normal way)

- **Multiple Listing Contract** (seller): s.1 lists the property exclusively with the brokerage; **s.7** designates the licensee(s) as the seller's sole agent(s). The brokerage itself is the seller's agent only for the limited purpose of **listing on MLS®**.
- **Buyer's Agency Exclusive Contract**: s.2 designates the buyer's agent(s). Some buyers balk at its mention of possibly paying their brokerage, so BCREA also offers the **Buyer Agency Acknowledgement**, which makes clear the buyer won't pay unless agreed.

**Rules s.43**: a brokerage acting for an **owner/seller** needs a **written service agreement**, unless the client **waives** it. (Buyer agreements aren't required by s.43, but they're prudent and many brokerages require them.) It must be signed by the **client** and an authorized signatory of the **brokerage** and include:

- the **parties** (owner and brokerage)
- the **property** (address, legal description, PID)
- the **term** and effective date
- the **services** and authority (list price, conditions of sale)
- **remuneration**: how much, when it's payable, and what the **cooperating brokerage** gets
- how **personal information** is handled

**Rules s.44**: give the client a **copy immediately** on signing (each co-owner should get one).

**Three types of listing:**

| Listing | How it works | Commission |
|---|---|---|
| **Exclusive** (in BC, usually an "exclusive **right to sell**") | One brokerage markets the property for the term | Payable on **any** sale during the term, **even if the owner finds the buyer**. If amended to let the owner sell commission-free, it becomes a true "exclusive listing". |
| **Multiple** | An exclusive right to sell **plus** MLS® listing and cooperation with other brokerages | As above; shared with the cooperating brokerage |
| **Open (general)** | Several brokerages can list at once | Only the brokerage that's the **effective cause** of the sale. Owner's own sale = **no** commission. The sale ends all the others' authority. |

### 2. Implied agency (by conduct)

An agency can arise from **how you behave**, even if nobody signed anything.

> 🚨 **Don't cross the line with unrepresented buyers.**
>
> **Allowed:** giving general market information and standard forms; helping them fill in forms (without advising on terms); presenting their offers and counter-offers.
>
> **Crosses the line:** **advising** them, **negotiating** for them, or revealing your client's **confidential** information (e.g. the seller's bottom line).
>
> Cross it and you may have an **implied agency**, owing fiduciary duties to **both** sides. That's impossible to honour, breaches the dual agency ban (**Rules s.63**), and invites lawsuits and discipline.

### 3. Agency by estoppel

Arises when the **principal** (by words or conduct) leads a third party to believe someone is their agent. Three requirements:

1. a clear **representation** by the principal
2. the third party **relies** on it
3. the third party **changes position** because of it

### 4. Agency by ratification

The principal **later approves** an act done without (or beyond) authority, and is bound as if it had been authorized from the start. **Never rely on it.** If the principal refuses, **you** may be personally liable.

## How much authority does the agent have?

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Express** (actual) | Spelled out in the agreement | Listing contract: advertise, show at reasonable hours, place signs, let cooperating brokerages show |
| **Implied** (actual) | Whatever is necessary and incidental to the express authority | Holding an **open house** may be implied in the authority to "show" the property |
| **Usual / customary** (actual) | Acting according to lawful, reasonable **customs of the trade**, unless told otherwise | Open houses are customary in the industry |
| **Apparent** | What a reasonable third party believes from the principal's conduct (estoppel) | Principal keeps letting a former agent deal for them |

A principal is bound only by acts within authority (or ratified). An agent who exceeds authority can be liable to the **principal** (breach of the agency contract) or to the **third party** (breach of warranty of authority).

### Signing for a client

1. **Written authorization first** (**Rules s.45**). Your listing or buyer agency agreement **doesn't** give it; get a **separate** written authority with clear limits. Talk to your managing broker.
2. **Sign correctly**: sign **your own name** and write that you're signing **as agent for** the client. Never copy the client's signature.

| Case | What happened | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Licensee signed 3 offers on **clear oral instructions** | Client later refused to complete, saying she had no authority | **Reprimand**, remedial education, **$1,250** expenses |
| Licensee **copied the client's signature** onto an addendum, without written authority | | **7-day suspension**, **$750** expenses |

## Ending an agency relationship

Agency is voluntary, so either side can end it. Three ways:

1. **Express termination**: oral or written notice (e.g. a frustrated seller emails "you're fired"). Return keys, take down signs and ads.
2. **By conduct**: something inconsistent with the agency continuing (e.g. the property **sells** with weeks left on the listing).
3. **Operation of law**: under a term of the contract, or on **death, mental incapacity or bankruptcy** of either side.

Ending a contract in a way it doesn't allow can still be a **breach**: a seller who cancels early may owe the brokerage **damages** (often not pursued, but possible). **Many consumers wrongly think they can cancel freely.**

**Multiple Listing Contract s.14**: the listing ends:

- on **expiry** of the term
- earlier by **mutual written agreement**
- on a **completed sale**
- **immediately** if the brokerage's licence is suspended, cancelled or inoperative
- on the brokerage's **bankruptcy, insolvency or receivership**
- if the brokerage must **stop acting** because of a conflict between current clients (Rules Part 5, s.65)

On termination, the brokerage must remove the MLS® listing, stop marketing, remove signs, and return the seller's documents if asked. (The Buyer's Agency Exclusive Contract **s.10** is similar.)

**After termination**, third parties may still think the agent has authority (apparent authority), so the principal should **notify** them.

## The agent and third parties

The agent isn't normally a party to the contract, **except** in three situations:

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| **Undisclosed agency**: the agent contracts in their own name | The third party can sue **the agent**, or the principal once revealed. (E.g. Al buys Blackacre in his own name for Sam, who then goes bankrupt. The seller can sue Al.) |
| **No authority / exceeding authority** | The agent is liable for **breach of warranty of authority** |
| **The agent commits a tort** (e.g. fraudulent misrepresentation) | The agent is **personally** liable. "My client told me to" is **no defence**. |

**Principals are liable for their agents' acts too.** Seller Simran tells Frankie to hide basement water problems; Frankie tells buyer Uma there are none. Uma can sue **Frankie** (fraud) **and** Simran.

> 🔎 **Even an honest principal pays.** Mr. Isley told his agent to disclose a past **cockroach** infestation; the agent didn't. The buyer can likely recover from **Isley**, who can then recover the full amount from the **agent**.

## The agent's duties to the principal

### Contractual duties

Follow the agency agreement. **Multiple Listing Contract s.8** says the designated agent will:

- act as agent for **only** the seller for this property (limited exceptions)
- give property information to cooperating brokerages
- use **reasonable commercial efforts** to market the property
- tell interested buyers, at the earliest reasonable opportunity, that they act for the seller
- fulfil **Rules s.30** duties
- obey the seller's **lawful** instructions
- use **reasonable care and skill**

### Tort duty: reasonable care and skill

- A seller's agent must do what a competent licensee would to get **fair market value**, and advise whether an offer is the **best reasonably obtainable**.
- A buyer's agent negotiates the **lowest price and best terms**.
- **Decline** work beyond your expertise (a residential agent selling an office tower) and refer it on.
- Rules: **s.30(d)** advise independent professional advice outside your expertise; **s.33** honesty; **s.34** reasonable care and skill.

### Agency duty: do it yourself

An agent must **personally** perform the work and can't delegate without permission. The client chose **you**. (The standard forms let the brokerage designate a **replacement** if you're suspended, unavailable or leave.)

### Fiduciary duties

Most agency relationships are **fiduciary** ("fiducia" = trust). Two special features:

- If a client alleges a breach, the **agent** must prove they met their duties.
- Most duties end with the relationship, but **confidentiality continues indefinitely**.

#### 1. Loyalty (the umbrella duty)

Act **solely** for the client's benefit, ahead of everyone, **including yourself**. Ask often: *Who is my client? Is this in their best interest? Is anyone else's interest influencing me?* Some of BCFSA's harshest penalties are for loyalty breaches.

#### 2. Avoid conflicts of interest

A conflict exists when there's a **substantial risk** that your representation will be **materially and adversely affected** by your own interests or duties to someone else (*Galambos v. Perez*, adopted for real estate). If a conflict exists: **fully disclose and get consent**, or **stop acting**.

Knowledge Base steps: **anticipate** conflicts, **talk** with clients about them **early and often**, and **disclose promptly and clearly** when they arise (sometimes you'll need to step aside).

**a) Remuneration conflicts (often unavoidable).** Commission-based pay creates temptation.

> ⚖️ Zdeno lists Zeus's home at $500,000 on Tuesday, with an open house planned for Saturday. A $495,000 offer arrives Wednesday, open 48 hours. Zdeno is tempted to push acceptance to save time and lock in a commission.
>
> **Right approach:** an honest assessment. The offer is fair, but the open house might bring more (even a bidding war); waiting also risks losing this offer and getting less. Then **Zeus decides**.

Buyer's agents face the mirror-image temptation: to push a higher price so the offer is accepted and they're paid. Have an **honest conversation about how you're paid** before you start.

**b) Conflicts between current clients (Rules s.65).** E.g. two of your buyer clients want the same property, or your buyer client wants your seller client's property. Unless the remote exception applies, you must **either**:

1. **stop acting for both** in that trade (and you **can't** keep helping either as an unrepresented party; refer them on), **or**
2. **continue for one only**, if **both** sign the **Agreement Regarding Conflict of Interest Between Clients** (a BCFSA form). It's signed **when the conflict arises**, not in advance.

| | **Continuing Client** | **Released Client** |
|---|---|---|
| Still represented? | Yes, full fiduciary duties | No. No advice; may get another agent or go unrepresented |
| Confidentiality | Can't receive the Released Client's confidential info (price limits, preferred terms) | **Still protected**: you can't disclose their confidential info to anyone |

It's an **agreement**, not just a disclosure: have a full conversation, make sure both understand the risks, and recommend independent advice.

> 🧭 **Showing your own listing to your buyer client.** Strictly a conflict, but BCFSA lets you **show** it and give **general factual information**, if you **disclose the conflict** first. If the buyer wants to **write an offer** or get advice, s.65 applies: stop acting for both, or one continues with both clients' written agreement. If either refuses to sign, you stop acting for **both**.

The Multiple Listing Contract and Buyer's Agency Exclusive Contract anticipate this: the seller agrees you may continue for the buyer (on other properties) or the brokerage may designate another agent or refer the seller out. Set expectations with clients **at the start** (e.g. "If you want one of my listings, I'll likely keep the seller and refer you"), but still get the **written agreement when it happens**.

**c) Conflicts with former clients.** A former client wants to buy your current seller's listing:

- Tell the former client you **can't represent** them.
- Ask whether their **confidential information** would impair you. Consider: **how long** ago? Was the info **transaction-specific** or general? Is the old deal **connected** to this one?
- If you continue, tell the seller the buyer is a **former client** and that you **can't share** what you know about them.

**d) Voluntary conflicts (higher risk).** Buying a client's property (for you or a relative/friend), selling your own property to a client, or taking **referral fees**. Best avoided. If you proceed: **full disclosure and consent**, plus **Rules s.53** (interest in trade) and **s.56** (other remuneration).

#### 3. Full disclosure

Disclose **all material information**: anything that might affect the property's value, the client's decision, or a reasonable person's choice. **Don't decide for the client** what's important. Be a **conduit**, not a **filter**. When in doubt, disclose.

**Secret profits** (undisclosed benefits from third parties) are forfeited to the client.

| Case | Facts | Result |
|---|---|---|
| ***Baillie v. Charman*** (1992 BCCA) | Listed at $299K; agent pushed a **$270K** offer from his **friend** without saying so; the contract was then assigned to the agent's own company, which resold for **$317K** | Breach of fiduciary duty. Agent paid **both commissions** (this sale and the friend's other purchase) **plus the $47K profit** |
| ***Ocean City Realty*** (1987 BCCA) | Licensee secretly agreed to split commission with the buyer (managing broker knew; the seller didn't) | Brokerage lost its **entire commission**. Disclose facts **good or bad for you** |
| **2021 BCSRE case** | Buyer's agent secretly agreed to pay the **seller $7,500** if they accepted a $2,615,000 offer from the agent's buyers, and didn't tell the buyers | Breach of **s.56(2)**: **14-day** suspension, **$5,000** penalty, **$1,500** expenses, remedial course |

#### 4. Confidentiality

Confidential = anything about the client **not public**.

| Seller's secrets | Buyer's secrets |
|---|---|
| Job transfer (time pressure) | Willing to pay more |
| Will take less than list | Already sold, must buy fast |
| Financial distress | Plans a quick resale (thinks it's underpriced) |
| | Identity, if they want anonymity (e.g. a celebrity) |

- Release only with the client's **express** (ideally written) permission. If unsure, **don't** disclose; ask.
- **Multiple Listing Contract s.7B**: the designated agent won't share the seller's confidential info even with **colleagues in the same brokerage** acting for buyers. **s.4B** authorizes publishing listing details, the PDS and, once firm, the sale price.
- **Forever.** In one case, after a listing **expired** and the home was relisted elsewhere, the former listing agent emailed buyers that the "extremely motivated" sellers would take **$300K below appraisal**: **7-day** suspension, **$1,000** expenses, remedial course.
- Don't confuse confidentiality with **Rules s.59**: **material latent defects** must be disclosed. If the seller forbids it, you **stop acting**.

#### 5. Obey lawful instructions

You **can't** follow instructions that would:

- break any **law**
- **misrepresent** the property
- **discriminate** on protected grounds (race, colour, ancestry, place of origin, religion, marital or family status, disability, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, age)

Tell the client the instruction is unlawful; if they insist, **stop acting**. Involve your managing broker.

### The Rules: s.30, s.33, s.34

Lesson 6 covered **Rules s.30** in detail: (a) best interests, (b) lawful instructions, (c) within authority, (d) independent advice, (e) confidentiality, (f) material information, (g) all offers promptly and objectively, (h) reasonable efforts to discover facts, (i) avoid conflicts, (j) disclose conflicts promptly, fully, **in writing**, **separately**.

- Many s.30 duties **mirror** common law duties, so one breach can mean a **lawsuit**, **BCFSA discipline** and **board** discipline.
- Even when ss.31–32 narrow s.30 to the designated agent, the **brokerage** must still supervise and protect every client's confidential information.
- **s.33**: act **honestly**, toward **everyone** (clients and non-clients).
- **s.34**: act with **reasonable care and skill**. A licensee copied **2,097 sq ft** from an old listing; it was really **1,672 sq ft**. Reprimand, remedial course, **$1,250** expenses.

## The principal's duties to the agent

**1. Comply with the agreement.** A seller who cancels early (outside the termination clause, without breach or frustration) may owe damages: advertising, staging, lost commission. Whether to sue is the brokerage's business decision.

**2. Pay the agent.** RESA **s.4**: you **can't sue** for remuneration if you weren't licensed (or exempt) when you provided the services.

Multiple Listing Contract **commission triggers**:

1. an enforceable CPS is signed **during the term**
2. an enforceable CPS with a buyer **introduced during the term**, signed **within 60 days after** expiry, **or at any time** where the brokerage or cooperating brokerage was an **effective cause** of the sale
3. a buyer **ready, willing and able** to pay the **list price and terms** makes an offer during the term, **even if the seller refuses to sign**

But **no commission** under triggers 1–2 if the buyer validly **rescinds under the HBRP**. The seller must also tell the agent about **all inquiries and offers**, even after expiry.

Key points:

- Check **what event** triggers commission, then whether it **happened**.
- The **brokerage**, not the licensee, is entitled to the commission (it signed the contract).
- No agreed amount → **quantum meruit** (often not worth suing for).
- Breach of duty can **forfeit** remuneration. BCFSA can also order a penalty **up to the remuneration** received.
- **RESA s.7**: licensees accept remuneration **only from their own brokerage**.

**Other remuneration rules:**

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **s.43(4)** | Seller service agreements state the remuneration, when it's payable, and the cooperating brokerage's share |
| **s.60** | **Net listings prohibited**. One licensee agreed to take whatever exceeded **$375,000** (max $5,000): reprimand, **$5,000** penalty, course, **$1,250** expenses |
| **s.66** | Don't pay anyone who **should be licensed and isn't**. **Exception**: a licensee from **another jurisdiction** (e.g. an Alberta agent who refers a buyer) can be paid |
| **ss.61–62** | How licensee remuneration is paid from brokerage accounts |
| **ss.56–57** | Remuneration disclosures (below) |

**3. Indemnify expenses?** Normally principals reimburse agents' expenses, but real estate agents are presumed to have **waived** this in exchange for commission, unless the contract says otherwise.

## Agency disclosure in BC

"**A fully informed consumer is a better protected consumer.**" Most complaints come from consumers who felt kept in the dark.

**Three ways to provide trading services:**

| Relationship | Who | Duties owed |
|---|---|---|
| **Sole agency** (most common, via designated agency) | You represent this party as a **client** and no one else in the trade | Full fiduciary duties + Rules ss.30, 33, 34 |
| **No agency** | Limited services to an **unrepresented party** (often while representing the other side) | No loyalty, no confidentiality, no full disclosure. Only honesty and care |
| **Dual agency** | Remote, under-served exception only | Greatly limited duties for both |

### Relationship disclosures

**s.54: Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services (DORTS).** Before providing trading services to **anyone**, disclose whether you'll represent them as a **client**. It's a prescribed, unalterable form (it replaced BCREA's *Working with a REALTOR®* brochure in 2018). It:

- contrasts being a **client** (loyalty, full disclosure, confidentiality) with being **unrepresented** (none of those)
- explains the **HBRP**
- states whether you'll represent them
- has an **optional acknowledgement**. Encourage signing; if they won't, note the date, time and circumstances on the form.

**Exceptions:** you needn't give it if you're **only** hosting an advertised **open house** or giving **factual answers to general questions**, **as long as** you don't ask about or receive their **motivation, finances or needs**.

> 🚨 **Listing presentations need a DORTS.** You'll hear the owner's motivations and plans, so give the form, give them a copy, and the brokerage keeps one as a record (Rules **s.83**), **even if you don't get the listing**.

**s.55: Disclosure of Risks to Unrepresented Parties.** Required when you give trading services to an **unrepresented** party **and** represent another party in the same trade. It's prescribed and unalterable. Its purposes:

1. explain the **risks**: you're loyal to your client, and **anything they tell you goes to your client**
2. list the **limited services** you can offer
3. **encourage** them to get their own representation

> 🔎 Garvin lists Jenna's home. Marek, unrepresented, wants Garvin to write his offer. Garvin gives Marek the **DORTS** and the **s.55** form, explains he can supply and help fill in the form and present the offer, but can't advise on price or terms, and suggests Marek get his own agent. Marek does.

**Disclosure for Residential Tenancies.** When acting for a **landlord** renting **residential** premises to an **unrepresented tenant**, one combined BCFSA form replaces both the DORTS and the s.55 form, and tells tenants where to get advice.

**s.64:** the dual agency disclosure (above). **s.65:** the conflict agreement (above). It's an **agreement**, not a disclosure.

### Remuneration disclosures

**s.56: Disclosure of Remuneration.** Disclose **promptly** to your client any remuneration you receive or expect from **anyone other than the client**, for:

1. providing services to that client (e.g. a **buyer's agent paid through the listing brokerage's split**), or
2. **recommending** (or being recommended by) a home inspector, mortgage broker, notary, lawyer, lender or other real estate-related provider (**referral fees**)

Disclose the **source**, **amount** (or how it's calculated) and relevant facts. BCFSA's form is **optional**, but get the client's signed acknowledgement.

> 🧭 **Referral fee example.** Matthew gets **$100** from inspector Jethro for every referral. Jethro isn't the best or the cheapest. Matthew must disclose the fee **when he recommends Jethro**, and loyalty requires his honest opinion of Jethro too. Deepak can then use Jethro anyway, ask for other names, or find his own.

> 🔎 **Developer bonuses (BCFSA guidance, 2022).** Offered a bonus if you sell X pre-sale units? Disclose the **potential** bonus to clients now (s.30(f), material information). Told **after** several sales that you've earned one? Go back and give **s.56** disclosure to **each** client whose purchase counted.

**s.57: Disclosure to Sellers of Expected Remuneration.** When **presenting an offer** to the seller, the seller's licensee must disclose, **in dollars**, using the **prescribed** form:

1. what the seller pays the listing brokerage (**gross**)
2. what goes to the **cooperating brokerage**
3. what the listing brokerage **keeps** (**net**)
4. any remuneration from **anyone else** (plus separate s.56 disclosure)

**Why?** Commission varies by offer, which creates temptation, especially **double-ending**.

> 🧮 **Worked example.** Seller Sven pays **7% on the first $100,000 + 3.5% on the balance**; the cooperating brokerage gets **3% + 1.5%**. Three offers at **$745,000**:
>
> - Gross commission: $7,000 + 3.5% × $645,000 ($22,575) = **$29,575**
> - Co-op share: $3,000 + 1.5% × $645,000 ($9,675) = **$12,675**
>
> | Offer | Listing brokerage keeps |
> |---|---|
> | #1: buyer with an agent | $29,575 − $12,675 = **$16,900** |
> | #2: buyer's brokerage promises a **$3,000 kickback** | **$19,900** |
> | #3: **unrepresented** buyer (double-ended) | **$29,575** |
>
> Same price, very different pay. That's the temptation the s.57 form exposes **when the seller is deciding**. (The listing contract disclosed rates weeks ago; this puts dollars in front of the seller now.)

### Other disclosures

**s.53: Disclosure of Interest in Trade.** Before **any agreement** is signed, disclose to the other party when **you**, or an **associate you're providing services to**, will directly or indirectly **acquire or dispose of** real estate.

- **Associate** includes your **spouse** (married or marriage-like), a **trust or estate** you have a substantial beneficial interest in, and a **corporation or partnership** where you or your spouse hold **5% or more**.
- **Rental exemption** (acquiring only): for **personal residential** use by you or your spouse, term **≤ 1 year** (including renewals), and **no option to purchase or right of first refusal**.
- Two prescribed forms (**Buying or Selling**; **Leasing or Renting**). When acquiring, disclose the **intended use** (personal, rental, resale and its terms) and the **remuneration** you or associates expect.
- Signed by the **licensee**, a **witness**, a **brokerage representative**, and the **recipient**.
- Recommended CPS clause: "The Buyer/Seller acknowledges having received and signed a disclosure of the licensee's interest in the transaction before the making/receipt of this offer."

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| ***Compar Services v. Foss*** (1983 BCSC) | Agent didn't disclose, **in the required written form before the offer**, that he was an officer and owner of the buying company. The seller could treat the contract as **void**, even though it knew he was an agent. |
| Licensee owned the **selling company** and didn't tell the buyer (5%+ interest) | **90-day** suspension, **$1,000** expenses |
| Disclosure given **after** the CPS was signed | Disciplined. It must come **before** |
| Licensee bought through **his own company** without disclosing, then advertised the property for resale by **assignment** without the owner's consent ("**shadow flipping**") | **$5,000** penalty, **$3,000** expenses, **45-day** suspension, **1 year** of enhanced supervision, remedial course. (Today the maximum penalty is **$250,000** per contravention.) |

> ⚠️ **No insurance for your own deals.** The E&O Indemnity Plan **excludes** claims where the insured has (or may acquire) an **ownership interest** in the property.

**s.57.1: HBRP disclosure** (Lesson 8): the buyer's licensee when **preparing** an offer, the seller's licensee when **presenting** one (BCFSA form, or the standard CPS); explain it.

**s.59: Material latent defects.** A licensee acting for a **seller** must disclose **known** material latent defects **in writing** to all other parties, **promptly** and **before** any agreement, **separately** from the contract (unless the seller already disclosed in writing). It's **broader** than common law: dangerous, unfit for habitation, unfit for a **known purpose**, **great expense** to fix, a **local government notice**, or **missing permits**.

> 🔎 Fiona tells her agent Rene about oil that seeped from the neighbouring auto shop into her yard, and an **unpermitted** basement suite, then says "don't tell buyers". Both are material latent defects. Fiduciary loyalty doesn't override s.59: if Fiona insists, Rene must **stop providing trading services** for this trade.

**Reg s.8.2: Assignment notice** (Lesson 8):

- **Buyer's side**: discuss the standard terms with the buyer; include them unless instructed otherwise **in writing**; if they're omitted, deliver the **Notice to Seller Regarding Assignment Terms** with the offer. Licensees buying for themselves must do the same.
- **Seller's side**: forward any Notice Form; if an offer lacks the standard terms, **discuss** its assignment terms with the seller.
- Both licensees give the notice to their brokerages, which keep it (**s.83(1)(d)**). Try to get the seller's signature as proof.

## Two alerts

> 🚨 **ALERT 1: Helping is fine; advising creates an agency.** With unrepresented parties, stick to **facts, forms and delivering offers**. The moment you **advise** or **negotiate** for them, or leak your client's bottom line, you may be their agent too: illegal dual agency and a lawsuit waiting.

> 🚨 **ALERT 2: Confidentiality never expires; disclosure obligations never sleep.** Former clients' secrets stay secret **forever**. But **material latent defects**, your **interest in a trade**, **referral fees** and **remuneration** must be disclosed **on time, in the right form**. Disclose the wrong thing, or the right thing too late, and the deal can be undone and you disciplined.

## Trap list: things the exam likes to twist

1. The agent **isn't a party** to the contract made for the principal (no privity).
2. A BC listing or buyer agency agreement **doesn't** authorize signing for the client: you need **separate written** authority (**s.45**), and you sign **your own name as agent**.
3. **Before 1994**: seller sub-agency. **1994**: assumed buyer agency. **June 2012**: designated agency. **2018**: dual agency banned.
4. Under **designated agency**, only the designated licensee(s) owe full duties; two designated agents in one brokerage on opposite sides is **not** dual agency.
5. **Brokerage agency** is the only model for brokerages doing **only** rental/strata management.
6. Rules **s.32**: the brokerage supervises, keeps confidences, and treats all clients **even-handedly**.
7. Dual agency includes **two buyers** for the same property, and lessor/lessee, assignor/assignee.
8. Dual agency exception (**s.64**): **remote + under-served + impracticable** (not just inconvenient); MB signs the form; written agreements; copies to **BCFSA**.
9. A **minor** can act as an agent (*Farajollahi*); a licensee must be **19+**.
10. **Rules s.43**: written service agreement for **owners/sellers** unless waived; **s.44**: copy to client **immediately**.
11. **Exclusive right to sell**: commission even if the **owner** finds the buyer. **Open listing**: only the **effective cause** gets paid; owner's own sale = no commission.
12. **Implied agency** from advising, negotiating for, or leaking confidences to an unrepresented party.
13. **Estoppel** needs representation **by the principal**, **reliance**, and **change of position**.
14. Ratification makes the principal bound **from the start**; if refused, the agent may be personally liable.
15. Express, implied and **usual/customary** authority are all **actual** authority; **apparent** authority comes from estoppel.
16. Termination: **express**, **conduct** (e.g. sale), **operation of law** (death, incapacity, bankruptcy).
17. Agents are personally liable to third parties for **undisclosed agency**, **breach of warranty of authority**, and their own **torts**.
18. A principal can be liable for an agent's failure to disclose **even after instructing** disclosure.
19. Fiduciary breach: the **agent** bears the burden of proof. **Confidentiality outlasts** the relationship.
20. Conflict between current clients (**s.65**): stop acting for **both** (and don't serve either as unrepresented) **or** continue for **one** with **both** clients' signed agreement **when the conflict arises**.
21. The **Released Client** still gets **confidentiality**.
22. Showing your own listing to a buyer client is allowed **with disclosure**; writing an offer triggers **s.65**.
23. **Secret profits** are forfeited; failure to disclose can cost the **whole commission** (*Ocean City*), plus profits (*Baillie v. Charman*).
24. **Commission belongs to the brokerage**, not the licensee. Licensees accept pay **only from their brokerage** (RESA s.7).
25. **No commission** if the buyer rescinds under the **HBRP**. Ready, willing and able buyer at list price and terms → commission **even if the seller refuses**.
26. **Net listings** are prohibited (**s.60**).
27. Paying an **out-of-province licensee** a referral is allowed (s.66 exception); paying an unlicensed person who should be licensed isn't.
28. Real estate agents are presumed to **waive** expense reimbursement.
29. **DORTS** exceptions: open house or general factual answers, **without** asking about motivation, finances or needs. **Listing presentations** need one.
30. The **s.55** form: risks, limited services, encouragement to get representation.
31. **s.56** covers commission splits paid to a buyer's agent **and** referral fees. The BCFSA form is **optional**.
32. **s.57**: at **offer presentation**, in **dollars**, gross / co-op / net / other.
33. **s.53** "associate" includes a company where you or your spouse hold **5%+**. Disclose **before** any agreement, or the contract may be **voidable** (*Compar v. Foss*).
34. E&O **excludes** claims where you have an **ownership interest**.

## Drill

**Q1.** You're the designated agent for a seller. At the open house, an unrepresented buyer asks, "What's the lowest the seller will take? And should I include a financing subject?" How do you respond?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

- You **can't** reveal the seller's bottom line (confidentiality, loyalty to the seller), and you **can't advise** the buyer on terms; doing so risks an **implied agency** and prohibited **dual agency** (s.63).
- Give the **DORTS** (you're now providing trading services beyond the open-house exception, since they're sharing needs) and the **Disclosure of Risks to Unrepresented Parties** form.
- Explain you represent the seller, anything they tell you goes to the seller, and you can only provide forms, factual information and present their offer.
- Suggest they get their **own representation**: another designated agent at your brokerage, or another brokerage.

</details>

**Q2.** Two of your buyer clients want to make offers on the same industrial unit (not your listing). What are your options?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

This is a conflict between current clients (two buyers for the same property counts as conflicting interests). Under **Rules s.65**:

1. **Stop acting for both** for this property and refer them to other licensees (you can't help either as an unrepresented party), **or**
2. **Continue acting for one**, but only if **both** sign the **Agreement Regarding Conflict of Interest Between Clients** now. The released client keeps **confidentiality** but gets no further advice from you.

If either refuses to sign, stop acting for both. Have a full conversation and recommend independent advice.

</details>

**Q3.** A seller signed a 90-day exclusive (right to sell) listing. In week 6 their neighbour offers to buy privately, and they sell without telling you. Is commission payable?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**Yes**, most likely. Under an exclusive **right to sell** (the standard Multiple Listing Contract), commission is payable if an enforceable contract is made **during the term**, regardless of who found the buyer. Only a true "exclusive listing" amended to let the owner sell commission-free, or an **open listing**, would change that. (The commission belongs to the **brokerage**, which decides whether to pursue it.)

</details>

**Q4.** Your buyer client asks you to sign an extension to their contract while they're overseas: "Just sign my name, I'm fine with it." What do you do?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

Don't sign their name. Get **written authorization** (Rules **s.45**), separate from the buyer agency agreement, setting out the specific purpose and limits. Then sign **your own name** with "as agent for [client]". Talk to your managing broker first. Licensees have been disciplined for acting on oral instructions (reprimand, course, $1,250) and for copying a client's signature (7-day suspension, $750).

</details>

**Q5.** You're presenting two equal $1.1M offers to your seller. One buyer is unrepresented. What must you disclose, and why does it matter here?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

Under **Rules s.57**, with **each** offer, give the **Disclosure to Sellers of Expected Remuneration** form showing, **in dollars**: the gross commission, the amount to any cooperating brokerage, what your brokerage keeps, and any other remuneration. Here the unrepresented buyer's offer means your brokerage **double-ends** and keeps far more. That's a conflict of interest, so the seller must see the numbers when deciding. Your advice must be based solely on the **seller's** best interests (price, terms, certainty), not your pay.

</details>

**Q6.** Your spouse owns 10% of a company that wants to buy a property listed by another brokerage, and asks you to write the offer. What must you do?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

The company is your **associate** (your spouse holds 5%+), and you're providing services to it, so **Rules s.53** applies. Before any agreement, give the seller (through their agent) the prescribed **Disclosure of Interest in Trade (Buying or Selling)** form: the relationship, intended use (personal, rental, resale terms) and any remuneration you or associates expect. It's signed by you, a witness, a brokerage representative and the recipient. Add the acknowledgement clause to the offer. Late or missing disclosure can make the contract **voidable** (*Compar v. Foss*) and lead to discipline. Remember your **E&O doesn't cover** claims where you have an ownership interest.

</details>

**Q7.** Your seller client instructs you: "Don't show the property to families with young kids, they'll wreck it." What do you do?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

That instruction is **unlawful**: discriminating on **family status** (and possibly age) breaches the *Human Rights Code*. You only obey **lawful** instructions. Explain this to the client, involve your managing broker, and if they insist, **stop acting** for them. You must also present **every** offer (s.30(g)).

</details>

## Mindset: you're a fiduciary first, a salesperson second

The law treats agents strictly because clients are **vulnerable**: they trust you with their biggest assets and their secrets. Every rule in this lesson comes back to three questions:

- **Who is my client?**
- **Am I acting in their best interest, not mine or anyone else's?**
- **Have I told everyone what the rules require, in the right form, at the right time?**

Licensees who get into trouble usually didn't set out to cheat. They **drifted**: a friendly chat with an unrepresented buyer, a referral fee they forgot to mention, a quick signature "to save time". Make disclosure a **habit**, not an afterthought.

> 🏢 **For commercial specifically.** Agency issues are sharper in commercial work:
>
> - The commercial community is **small**, so you'll often be asked to act against **former clients** or for **two clients** chasing the same building. Know s.65 cold.
> - **Open listings** and non-exclusive arrangements are common, so "effective cause" disputes arise.
> - **Corporate clients** may want you to sign documents: insist on **written** authority from someone who can bind the company.
> - **Land assemblies** often use agents buying without naming the principal. Remember an agent for an **undisclosed** principal can be **personally liable**, and s.53 applies if you or an associate are involved.
> - **Landlord representation** in leasing is agency too. Every disclosure rule applies, and the unrepresented-tenant rules apply to **commercial** tenants through the standard DORTS and s.55 forms.

> **This week's habit.** Download BCFSA's **DORTS** and **Disclosure of Risks to Unrepresented Parties** forms. Practise explaining each one out loud in under two minutes, in plain English, as if to a nervous first-time buyer, then to a sophisticated commercial investor.
