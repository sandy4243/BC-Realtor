## Why strata documents are where the surprises hide

> **Scenario.** Your buyer client loves a $780,000 condo in a 30-year-old building. The seller's Property Disclosure Statement says "no known leaks". Your buyer wants a quick, subject-free offer.
>
> - Buried in last year's **council minutes**: an engineer's report on the **building envelope**, and a plan to vote on a **$2.4 million special levy** at the next general meeting.
> - The **depreciation report** shows the contingency reserve fund is badly underfunded.
> - The **bylaws** were amended six months ago to ban **dogs**. Your client has a dog. (Luckily, existing pets are grandfathered, but only pets living there **when** the bylaw passed.)
>
> If your client buys without reading those documents, they could owe tens of thousands of dollars within months, and the seller may not be liable, because the information was **available** to them. And you may be disciplined for not reviewing it.

A strata lot isn't just an apartment. It comes with a **corporation** your client will automatically join, its **finances**, its **bylaws**, its **lawsuits** and its **repair bills**. This lesson explains how stratas are governed and financed, what to check before your client buys, how **leasehold** stratas differ, and how **cooperatives** compare.

## Vocabulary first

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Strata lot** | A privately owned unit (held in fee simple) shown on a strata plan. |
| **Common property (CP)** | Everything on the strata plan that isn't a strata lot (hallways, roof, grounds), plus pipes and wires in boundary walls, owned by all owners as **tenants in common**. |
| **Limited common property (LCP)** | Common property set aside for the **exclusive use** of one or more strata lots (e.g. a patio or parking stall). |
| **Common assets** | Property the strata corporation owns (a lawn mower, a caretaker's suite). |
| **Unit entitlement** | Each lot's number on the Schedule of Unit Entitlement, used to divide **ownership of CP** and **expenses**. |
| **Strata corporation** | The legal entity created when the strata plan is deposited. All owners are members. |
| **Strata council** | The elected group that runs the corporation between meetings, like a board of directors. |
| **AGM / SGM** | Annual general meeting / special general meeting of owners. |
| **Contingency reserve fund (CRF)** | Savings for expenses that happen **less than once a year** (roof, elevator, envelope). |
| **Special levy** | A one-time "cash call" on owners. |
| **Depreciation report** | A study of major components, their remaining life and replacement cost, with a 30-year forecast. |
| **Bylaws / rules** | The strata's constitution (passed by owners) / less formal regulations about **common property** (made by council). |
| **Form B / Form F / Form K** | Information Certificate / Certificate of Payment / Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities. |
| **Section** | A "mini strata corporation" for a group of similar lots (e.g. the commercial units in a mixed-use building). |
| **Leasehold strata** | A strata on **leased** land; buyers acquire a long-term **lease**, not fee simple. |
| **Cooperative** | A corporation that owns the building; members own **shares** and lease their unit. |

## The strata concept and unit entitlement

BC has had strata law since **1966**. The ***Strata Property Act*** replaced the *Condominium Act* on **July 1, 2000**.

When a developer deposits a strata plan, the land is divided into **strata lots** (each owned in fee simple) and **common property** (owned by all owners as tenants in common).

**Unit entitlement** does two jobs:

1. sets each owner's **share of ownership** of the common property
2. sets each lot's **share of expenses** (strata fees, special levies, judgments)

It's shown in the **Schedule of Unit Entitlement** (**Form V**), filed with the strata plan. For residential lots it's usually based on **habitable area** (excluding patios, balconies, garages, parking and storage other than closets). Other bases may apply to non-residential lots, or plans filed before July 1, 2000.

> 🧮 **The formula.** A lot's share of an expense = (its unit entitlement ÷ total unit entitlement of all lots) × the total expense.
>
> Example: lot 12 has unit entitlement **85**; all lots total **10,000**. A **$100,000** special levy costs lot 12: 85 ÷ 10,000 × $100,000 = **$850**.

**Licensee tips:**

- Check the Schedule (via **myLTSA** or the land title office) so you can explain fees and levies accurately.
- Expenses can be split another way if the owners pass a **unanimous** resolution and **register** it. Check the **General Index** (kept for each plan since 2000) for such resolutions.
- Plans filed **before July 1, 2000** have a **Schedule of Interest on Destruction** (how insurance money is split if the building is destroyed and not rebuilt). Newer plans use conversion or interest schedules on winding up.

## Common property and who gets to use it

**Common property** includes the parts of the land and buildings not in any strata lot, **plus** pipes, wires, cables, ducts and similar services that run through a boundary floor, wall or ceiling, or that run through a lot but serve other lots or the CP.

Every owner may use CP, subject to the Act, the bylaws and the rules. The **strata corporation** controls, manages, repairs and maintains it.

There are two ways to give someone **exclusive use** of common property:

| | **Limited common property (LCP)** | **Short term exclusive use** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | CP designated for the exclusive use of one or more lots | A permission or "special privilege" to use CP (or a common asset) exclusively |
| Attached to | The **strata lot** (runs with the land) | The **owner or tenant** personally |
| Created by | Developer on the strata plan; or owners: **unanimous** vote to amend the plan, or a **¾ vote** resolution filed at the LTO (shown in the **Common Property Record**, not on the plan) | **Council majority** decision. A **significant change** in use or appearance needs a **¾ vote** of owners (only a **majority** if it's for EV charging) |
| Length | Permanent until removed (by the same type of vote) | Max **1 year** (renewable), or **5 years** for an EV-charging stall; cancellable on reasonable notice |

The owner using LCP must give the strata reasonable access to do its job.

### Parking stalls and storage lockers

These can be held in several different legal ways. Know which applies before you describe them to a buyer:

- a **separate strata lot** (no longer allowed in residential plans, but found in some older ones)
- **part of** a strata lot
- **LCP** (on the strata plan, or by filed resolution)
- **common property** used under a short term exclusive use arrangement, a licence or lease from the strata, or rights the **developer** leased and then sold or assigned to individuals

### EV charging

- An owner can ask the strata to approve changes to CP needed to install **EV charging** at a stall (s.90.1), with details like cost and timing. The strata must decide within **3 months** and **can't unreasonably refuse**, though it can impose reasonable written conditions (approved contractors, materials, replacing the equipment later).
- Approvals usually needing a **¾ vote** (significant change to CP, buying personal property, spending from the CRF) drop to a **majority** when the purpose is **EV charging** or managing its electricity.

## The strata corporation

**Created** when the strata plan is deposited at the land title office (s.2). Its legal name is "**The Owners, Strata Plan [number]**", and it has the legal capacity of a natural person (it can contract, sue and be sued).

> ⚠️ **Not like a company.** Shareholders of a company aren't personally liable for its debts. But a **judgment against a strata corporation is a judgment against every owner** (s.166), shared by unit entitlement. An owner who **sued** the strata and won doesn't contribute to paying their own judgment, or to the strata's defence costs.

**Sections** (s.191) are "mini strata corporations" for groups of lots with different interests: **residential vs non-residential**, different commercial uses, or **townhouses vs apartments**. Each section has its own executive and can:

- bear costs that relate only to its own lots (e.g. commercial owners avoid paying for the residential elevator and window cleaning)
- make its own decisions on matters it's responsible for

The strata corporation still handles matters of common interest, and at least the **insurance**.

### The strata corporation's duties

| Duty | What it involves |
|---|---|
| **Manage** (s.3) | Common property and common assets, for the owners' benefit |
| **Repair and maintain** | Common property and common assets |
| **Insure** | **Full replacement value** property insurance on CP, buildings on the plan, common assets, and fixtures the developer installed in lots; plus **liability** insurance for property damage and bodily injury |
| **Enforce** | Bylaws and rules (through council) |
| **Keep records** | Minutes, lists of owners, tenants, mortgagees, books of account, and more |

> 💡 **Insurance gaps.** The strata's policy won't cover an owner's **contents**, special levies when the strata's coverage falls short, or **deductibles** the bylaws pass on to an owner (which can be large). Advise buyers to talk to an **insurance broker** before removing subjects.

### Records and who can see them

**s.35** requires records such as: minutes of general and council meetings (with vote results), council members, owners (with addresses, lot numbers, stalls, lockers, unit entitlements), mortgagees who've filed a request for notice, **tenants**, assignments of rights to tenants, and **books of account**.

**s.36**: records must be available to:

- an **owner**
- a **tenant** who has been assigned the owner's rights
- **anyone authorized in writing** by an owner (or such a tenant)

How tenants get an owner's rights:

- **s.148**: a residential tenant on a lease of **3 years or more** is automatically assigned the landlord's powers and duties (after giving the strata **written notice**).
- **s.147**: an owner can assign some or all powers to a tenant by **written notice** to the strata stating the tenant's name, the powers and the time period.

**Timing:** copies within **2 weeks** (bylaws and rules within **1 week**). Set subject removal dates to allow for this.

> ⚠️ Posting strata records online without following the Act's requirements can breach the Rules and lead to discipline.

## What the buyer can get, and what you must read

A "**purchaser**" under the Act is someone who has **agreed** to buy, even if subjects remain. Purchasers aren't yet owners, but they can get information three ways:

| Route | Contents | Timing and cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Information Certificate (Form B)** (s.59) | Monthly fees; amounts the owner owes; agreements making the owner responsible for alterations; **approved future special levies**; expected budget overruns; **CRF balance** (less approved spending); unfiled bylaw amendments and resolutions; winding-up resolutions; **notices of upcoming ¾, 80% or unanimous resolutions** or bylaw changes; **court, arbitration or tribunal proceedings** and judgments; outstanding **work orders**; allocated **parking and storage**; an **insurance summary**; any **electrical planning report**. Attached: **rules**, current **budget**, most recent **depreciation report**. | Within **8 days**; max **$35** plus **25¢/page** |
| **Certificate of Payment (Form F)** (s.115) | Whether money is owed: strata fees, special levies, work-order costs, a share of a judgment, **fines**, bylaw-remedy costs. Not unproven claims. | Within **1 week**; max **$15**. Valid **60 days**. Needed to **register** a transfer (except court-ordered sales). Usually ordered by the buyer's **lawyer or notary** near completion |
| **Through the seller** (contract term) | The seller authorizes the buyer (and their licensee) **in writing** to inspect and copy the strata's records | **No inspection fee**; copies max **25¢/page** |

> 🔎 ***Sask v. Brooke*** (2000 BCSC). A buyer paid **$133,000** for a condo, subject to reviewing council minutes, bylaws and financial statements. After completion: a **$60,000** special levy for leaks, and the unit's value fell to **$22,000**. The seller's disclosure statement wrongly said there were no leaks, but the **minutes** described the leak history. The buyer's claim **failed**: she had the documents, so she couldn't show **reasonable reliance** on the seller's statement. **If a subject says "review the documents", read them.**

**What licensees should review** (at least the last **2 years** where available):

- **council minutes**
- **AGM and SGM minutes**
- minutes of any **section** the lot belongs to
- the **budget and financial statements**
- the **bylaws and rules**

Also: the **depreciation report**, Form B, engineering and envelope reports, and the insurance summary.

> 🔎 **Discipline (2019).** A buyer's agent didn't review strata documents mentioning an imminent **roof replacement**, didn't obtain all the available documents, and recommended a **subject-free** purchase. The buyer then received a **$17,402** special levy. **Reprimand for professional misconduct**, plus penalties.

> 🔎 **Earlier case.** A licensee failed to disclose major **water and structural damage** to the building envelope; the buyer's agent was also found **negligent** for not reading the minutes supplied under a subject clause.

## How a strata is governed

### The council

Elected by owners; exercises the strata corporation's powers and duties, **including enforcing the bylaws and rules** (s.26). The bylaws set the number of council members.

- **Eligibility**: owners (only one co-owner per lot at a time unless all are on council; one representative for a corporate owner). Tenants in some cases.
- **Standard of conduct (s.31)**: act **honestly and in good faith** in the strata's best interests, with the **care, diligence and skill of a reasonably prudent person**, regardless of inexperience.
- A council member acting honestly and in good faith isn't personally liable (Standard Bylaw 22), and the strata can buy E&O insurance for them.

### The developer's first council and the handover

- The **developer** acts as the first council, with the same duties of honesty, good faith and care (s.6).
- **First AGM (s.16)**: the developer must call it within **6 weeks** of the earlier of: **50% + 1** of lots conveyed, or **9 months** after the first conveyance.
- Late? The developer pays the strata **$1,000** for the first 30 days, and **$1,000** for each further 7 days. If the developer never calls it, an **owner** can.
- At the first AGM, owners elect the first owner council. A new council is elected at **every AGM**.

### General meetings

- **Notice**: at least **20 days'** written notice of every general meeting.
- **AGM**: every year, within **2 months** after fiscal year-end. Elects council and approves the **budget**.
- **SGM** (formerly "EGM"): called by council at any time, or **demanded** in writing by holders of at least **20%** of the votes (their item goes first on the agenda).
- Meetings can be held **electronically** if the notice explains how, everyone can communicate, and the chair can verify eligible voters.
- Owners can waive a meeting or its notice by following the Act's procedures.

### Voting

| Threshold | Typical uses |
|---|---|
| **Majority** (> 50%) | Default; budget; ratifying rules; **EV-charging** related approvals; CRF spending for a **depreciation report** or items it recommends; hiring a brokerage to market the whole complex (*Dubas*) |
| **¾ vote** | **Bylaw amendments** (after the second AGM, residential); **special levy** by unit entitlement; spending from the CRF; LCP by resolution; significant change to CP; operating-fund spending outside the budget |
| **80%** | **Winding up** the strata (plus a **court order**) |
| **Unanimous** | Amending the **strata plan** (e.g. LCP that way); special levy **not** by unit entitlement; changing the expense formula; residential bylaw changes **before the second AGM** |

- Co-owners share one vote. If they can't agree, the vote **isn't counted**.
- Only owners vote, unless they've assigned the vote to a **tenant**, or a **mortgagee** with that right in the mortgage gives **3 days'** written notice (it can vote only on insurance, maintenance, finance or matters affecting its security).
- Voting age is **16**; a parent or guardian votes for a younger owner; someone legally authorized (e.g. under an enduring POA) votes for an incapable owner.

### Strata managers

A strata manager paid to provide strata management services must be **licensed** under RESA (unless exempt). They represent the **strata corporation**, not individual owners, take instructions **only from council**, and owe the corporation **fiduciary** duties.

## Strata finances

Every strata has **two funds**, accounted for separately, both paid for through **strata fees** (allocated by unit entitlement):

| | **Operating fund** | **Contingency reserve fund (CRF)** |
|---|---|---|
| Pays for | Expenses that happen **once a year or more often** | Expenses that happen **less than once a year**, or rarely |
| Can be spent if… | **In the budget**; or **¾ vote**; or within council's bylaw limit (if the bylaws are silent: the **lesser of 5%** of the year's operating contributions **or $2,000**); or an **emergency** (minimum needed) | **¾ vote** (only a **majority** for a depreciation report, items it recommends, EV charging, or an electrical planning report); or an **emergency** (including keeping required **insurance**), minimum needed, owners told ASAP |
| Annual contribution | Per budget | At least **10%** of the operating fund budget, considering the latest depreciation report |

**Owners can't get their CRF contributions back** when they sell (s.101).

Owners and tenants must be told **as soon as feasible** about any material change in insurance, such as a higher **deductible**.

### The developer's interim budget

- Before the **first conveyance**, the developer pays all expenses.
- From the first conveyance until the first AGM, the developer's **interim budget** applies: estimated operating expenses, a CRF contribution, and each lot's monthly share. A copy goes to **every prospective purchaser**. The developer pays fees on **unsold** lots.
- **Shortfall?** The developer pays the difference. If it's more than **10%** of the estimate, the penalty is **double (or triple)** the shortfall.
- **Surplus?** Refunded to owners, unless refunds would be **$100 or less** each (then it can go to the CRF).
- Owners approve their first annual budget (majority) at the first AGM. Any year-end **deficit** must be eliminated the next year.

**Seeding the CRF.** At the first conveyance, the developer pays a lump sum into the CRF: the **lesser of** 10% of the interim budget's operating expenses × the number of years since the plan was deposited, **or** 50% of those expenses. (First conveyance within a year: **10%**, e.g. $10,000 on a $100,000 budget.) Under the old *Condominium Act* there was no CRF requirement, which left many older buildings short.

For stratas created on or after **July 1, 2027** with **5+ lots**, the developer must also fund the **first depreciation report**: the lesser of **$30,000** or **$5,000 + $200 per lot**, by the first AGM.

### Special levies

| How it's shared | Vote needed |
|---|---|
| By **unit entitlement** | **¾ vote** |
| Any **other** basis | **Unanimous** |

- The resolution must state the **purpose**, total, the method and each lot's share, and the **due date(s)**. Interest on late payments only if the resolution or bylaws allow it.
- If a ¾ vote fails for a repair that's **necessary for safety or to prevent significant loss**, the strata can pass it by **majority** and apply to the **Supreme Court** within **90 days** for approval (s.173(2)).
- Levy money is kept **separate** and used only for its purpose; owners are told how it was spent. Surplus goes back to **current** owners (or the CRF if nobody would get more than **$100**).
- **Who pays when a lot is sold? (s.109)** Whoever is the **owner when a payment falls due**. The seller pays instalments due before completion; the buyer pays those due after.

> 🧭 **Protect the buyer.** If a levy may be approved before completion, use the **Knowledge Base** clause making the seller responsible for it. A **holdback** clause is another option: use the wording of **s.108** ("**approved**", not "passed"; "**special levy**", not "assessment"), and say what happens if no levy is approved by a set date. Talk to a lawyer before using one.

**Strata financing.** For big repairs, stratas increasingly use **loans** (often like a line of credit, paid directly to contractors) instead of a huge levy.

### Depreciation reports and electrical planning reports

**Depreciation reports (s.94)** became mandatory in **December 2011**.

- Prepared by a **qualified person**, after an **on-site visual inspection**.
- Contents: component inventory and evaluation; summary of repairs; **30-year** financial forecast and its effect on the CRF; preparer's details (qualifications, insurance, relationship to the strata); date; executive summary.
- **Timing**: new stratas formed on or after **July 1, 2027**: within **18 months** of the first AGM; formed July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2027: within **2 years** of the first AGM; older stratas: dates set by regulation. Then every **5 years**.
- **Exempt**: stratas with **fewer than 5 lots**.

**Electrical planning reports (s.94.1)**: required for stratas with **5+ lots**, by **Dec 31, 2026** in Metro Vancouver, the Fraser Valley and the Capital Regional District (excluding the Gulf Islands), and by **Dec 31, 2028** elsewhere; new stratas within **5 years** of deposit. Prepared by a professional engineer, applied science technologist or journeyperson electrician. Attached to **Form B**.

### Collecting money from owners

- First, at least **2 weeks' written demand** before suing, arbitrating or going to the **Civil Resolution Tribunal**.
- The strata can register a **lien** on the lot for: strata fees, special levies (with interest), reimbursement for work it did that the owner was responsible for, the lot's share of a judgment, and the costs of filing.
- **No lien** for **fines** or bylaw-remedy costs. For those, the strata sues (within the **2-year** limitation period) or simply **withholds the Form F** until they're paid when the owner sells.
- No lien if the owner pays the disputed amount into court or trust, or makes payment arrangements.
- A strata lien ranks **ahead of every other charge**, including mortgages, except: a lien for the lot's share of a judgment against the strata, Crown liens (other than mortgages), and **builders liens**.
- The strata can sue, register the judgment, and apply for a **court-ordered sale**.

> ⚠️ **Buying in a strata forced sale.** The strata, as seller, gives few of the usual assurances (condition, residency). The buyer's lawyer should make sure the **vesting order** gives title **free and clear**.

## Bylaws and rules

| | **Bylaws** | **Rules** |
|---|---|---|
| What | The strata's **constitution**: use of lots, CP and assets, and administration | Optional; regulate use, safety and condition of **CP and common assets** only, **not strata lots** |
| Made by | **Owners** (vote) | **Council** |
| Effective | Amended bylaws are enforceable only once **filed at the LTO** | Must be **ratified by majority** at the next AGM (or an earlier SGM), or they lapse |
| If they conflict | Bylaws prevail | |

- The **Standard Bylaws** (Schedule to the Act) apply to **every** strata (all of them since **January 1, 2002**), except where a strata validly filed its own bylaws on the same subject before that date. Stratas can amend them.
- Bylaws and rules are **unenforceable** if they breach the Act, the regulations, the ***Human Rights Code*** or any other law, even if passed unanimously.
- Bylaws generally can't restrict an owner's right to **sell, lease or mortgage** (but see age restrictions below).

**Bylaw amendment thresholds:**

| Strata type | Before the 2nd AGM | At or after the 2nd AGM |
|---|---|---|
| Residential | **Unanimous** | **¾ vote** |
| Non-residential | ¾ (or as the bylaws say) | ¾ (or as the bylaws say) |
| Mixed | **Unanimous** | **¾ of residential AND ¾ of non-residential** lots (or as the bylaws say for non-residential) |
| Bare land | **Unanimous** | As for its type above |

(Before the first conveyance, the developer can simply file amended bylaws.)

**Grandfathering:** an **age** restriction doesn't apply to someone living there when it passes, and a **pet** ban doesn't apply to a pet living there when it passes.

### Enforcing bylaws and rules

Due process first:

1. receive a **complaint**
2. give the owner or tenant **written particulars** and a **reasonable chance to answer**, including a **hearing** if requested (and notify a tenant's **landlord**)
3. give a **written decision** promptly

Then the strata can:

- **fine**. Standard Bylaws: **$50** per bylaw breach, **$10** per rule breach. Regulation maximums: **$200** per bylaw breach and **$50** per rule breach, every **7 days** for continuing breaches. For **short-term rental** (vacation accommodation) bylaws: up to **$1,000** per breach, **daily**.
- do the **remedial work** and charge the cost
- deny use of a **recreational facility** for a reasonable time

**Landlords are vicariously liable** for their tenants' fines and remedy costs (and can recover them from the tenant).

> 🔎 ***Bahmutsky v. Petkau*** (2020 BCCRT). A neighbour's constant smoking in her LCP courtyard was a **nuisance** under the Standard Bylaws. The strata threatened a fine but never followed through. Because enforcing bylaws is **mandatory** (s.26), the strata had to pay half of **$1,000** in damages.

### Common restrictions to check

**Rentals.** Since **November 24, 2022**, stratas **can't** restrict rentals. They can't screen or approve tenants or require lease terms. **Short-term rental** bans are still allowed. Before renting a residential lot, the landlord must give the tenant the **bylaws and rules** and a **Form K** (Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities), signed by the tenant and given to the strata within **2 weeks** of the tenancy starting. If not, the tenant can end the tenancy within **90 days** of finding out, with the landlord paying moving costs up to **one month's rent**.

**Age.** Only **55+** age restrictions are now allowed (other ages, such as 19+, were removed in **2022**). They don't breach the *Human Rights Code* because the Act specifically allows them. Exemptions: residents there when it passed, **caregivers**, and the children and younger spouses of qualifying residents.

> 🔎 ***Strata Plan NWS3075 v. Stevens*** (2018 BCSC). Ms. Stevens inherited her mother's unit by **right of survivorship** and moved in, but was under 55. Fines of **$200/week**, over **$13,000**, were upheld.

> 🔎 **Discipline.** A listing licensee relied on the seller and another agent saying there was **no age bylaw**, supplied **outdated** bylaws, and missed minutes showing a ¾ vote adopting one. The buyer had to sell. **14-day suspension**, two strata law courses, and costs. **Never rely on signage, the presence of children, or hearsay. Read the current bylaws and recent minutes.**

**Pets.** The Standard Bylaw allows a reasonable number of fish/small aquarium animals, small caged mammals, up to **2 caged birds**, and **one dog or one cat**. Many stratas vary this. **Guide and service dogs** (including retired ones) are always allowed (s.123(3)). Ask buyers about pets early.

**Cannabis.** Stratas may restrict smoking and growing, but limits on **medical** cannabis can raise human rights issues.

**Future uses.** Bylaws can change **after** you buy.

> 🏢 ***Kunzler v. Strata Plan EPS 1433*** (2020 BCSC, aff'd 2021 BCCA). Buyers of a **16-acre** bare land strata lot told the strata they planned **cannabis production**. Within about a month, owners passed a bylaw banning it. The court said it wasn't **significantly unfair**: owners can't expect bylaws never to change. If your client's purchase depends on a particular **use**, raise it with the strata **before** buying.

## Repairs

Who **does** the work and who **pays** for it can differ.

**Who does the work (Standard Bylaws):**

| Area | Responsible |
|---|---|
| **Common property** | Strata corporation (s.72) |
| **LCP**: repairs and maintenance that usually happen **less than once a year** | Strata corporation |
| **LCP and strata lots**: always, however often: the building's **structure** and **exterior**; chimneys, stairs, **balconies** and other exterior attachments; **exterior doors, windows and skylights** (or those fronting CP); **fences and railings** around patios, balconies and yards | Strata corporation |
| Other LCP repairs (routine) | Owner |
| Everything else in the strata lot | Owner |

**Who pays:** generally **all** lots by unit entitlement, with some exceptions (e.g. sections). The strata is usually liable for damage **inside** a lot only if it came from CP **and** the strata didn't act reasonably in maintaining it.

**Alterations.** Owners usually need the strata's **consent** to alter their lot, and often sign an **indemnity agreement** that can bind future buyers. Unapproved alterations may have to be **removed** by the new owner. Check **Form B** and attached agreements.

## Winding up and legal proceedings

**Winding up.** Older buildings with high repair costs on valuable land may be sold as a whole to a developer. That requires an **80% vote** at a general meeting **and** a **BC Supreme Court** order (ss.272–289).

> 🏢 ***Dubas v. Strata Plan VR 92*** (2019 BCCA). Hiring a brokerage to **market** the complex to developers needed only a **majority** vote, not 80%. Strata wind-ups are a significant source of **commercial land assembly** work.

**Legal proceedings.**

- The strata can sue anyone, including owners, and can seek court orders to make people comply with the Act, bylaws and rules.
- Anyone can sue the strata about CP, common assets, bylaws, rules, or its acts. **All owners share** the defence costs and any judgment, **except** the owner who sued.
- **s.165**: an owner, tenant, **mortgagee** or other interested person can seek an order forcing the strata to do its duties.
- **s.164**: an owner or tenant can ask the court to stop or remedy **significantly unfair** actions by the strata or council, or by someone holding **50%+** of the votes.
- Many disputes go to the **Civil Resolution Tribunal**; arbitration and court-appointed **administrators** are also possible.

## Leasehold stratas

Most stratas are **freehold**: buyers get fee simple. In a **leasehold strata** (Part 12 of the Act), the developer **leases** land from a **leasehold landlord**:

- the governments of **BC or Canada**, a **municipality**, a **regional district**
- the **Nisga'a** Nation or a Nisga'a Village, or a **treaty First Nation** (Tsawwassen, Maa-nulth, Tla'amin)
- another listed **public authority** (e.g. a **university**, a school board, the Sechelt Indian Band, the Provincial Rental Housing Corporation)

**How it works:**

1. The developer signs a **ground lease** of at least **50 years**, registered on the landlord's title, with a model **strata lot lease** attached.
2. Depositing the **leasehold strata plan** creates fee simple titles for each lot **in the landlord's name**, and converts the ground lease into individual **strata lot leases**.
3. The developer **assigns** each strata lot lease to a buyer, who becomes the **leasehold tenant** (registered as a charge on the lot's title).
4. Any **Schedule of Restrictions** filed with the plan (limits on leasing, assignment, occupancy) binds everyone. **Always check for one.**

**Owning one:** leasehold tenants pay **strata fees**, **levies** and **property taxes**, and may also owe **rent** under the lease (prepaid, or ongoing with **rent reviews** that can jump sharply). The interest is a **wasting asset**: each year, less term remains.

**At the end of the term:**

- The landlord must give at least **1 year's** notice of whether it will renew. **No notice = automatic 5-year renewal.**
- A renewal must be at least **5 years**, on the same terms except rent (set under s.211, open to arbitration) and length.
- If it **doesn't renew**, the landlord must **buy** the tenant's interest: by the lease's formula, or a schedule filed with the plan, or otherwise **fair market value** "as if the lease didn't expire" (no regulation sets the formula; disputes go to arbitration).
- The landlord can instead **convert to freehold** by selling fee simple to **every** leasehold tenant (s.216).

> ⚠️ **Unsettled law.** The **City of Vancouver** (landlord of several False Creek South leaseholds) says the buyout value **excludes the land**, only the improvements. Many leaseholders disagree, and no court has decided it. Don't advise on this; send clients to a lawyer.

**Four key principles:**

1. The buyer gets a **leasehold interest** with a limited life, not fee simple.
2. There may be **rent**, possibly rising.
3. The **landlord alone** decides whether to renew.
4. The payout on termination likely **isn't fee simple value**.

**Licensee checklist:** search title and get the **strata lot lease** every time; make sure both sides understand what's being bought and sold; set price with the **remaining term** in mind; recommend **legal advice**; check what the landlord and lender will require (financing can be harder). If you lack the expertise, you may need to **decline** or get help (care and skill), and refer legal, tax and appraisal questions out (**Rules s.30(d)**).

> 🚨 **No HBRP.** The Home Buyer Rescission Period **doesn't apply** to leasehold interests or homes on leased land. Tell buyers.

## Strata vs cooperative

In a **cooperative**, a corporation (now usually under the ***Cooperative Association Act***, formerly the *Society Act* or *Company/Business Corporations Act*) owns the land and building (in fee simple or on a long lease). Buyers purchase **shares** allocated to a unit, which entitle them to **lease** that unit.

- Shares are usually allocated by value: a unit with **2 of 50** shares is about **4%** of the project's value.
- The co-op is the **landlord**; members are its **tenants**.
- **Financing** is harder: the buyer can only offer the lender a security interest in **shares** (under the *Personal Property Security Act*) and, if allowed, a mortgage of the lease. Rates may be higher.
- Some co-ops have a **blanket mortgage** on the whole property: members' payments include a share of it, building equity over time. The risk: if some members **stop paying**, the rest must cover it, or the co-op faces **foreclosure**.
- Sales usually need **board approval** of the buyer, and boards have wide discretion (approval has been refused for reasons unrelated to money).
- **REDMA** disclosure applies to co-op developments (2+ cooperative interests), and residential tenants displaced by a co-op conversion keep **RTA** protections.
- The **HBRP does apply** to a cooperative interest that includes a right to occupy a dwelling. If unsure, consult your managing broker.

| | **Fee simple strata** | **Cooperative** |
|---|---|---|
| Created by | Depositing a **strata plan** at the LTO | **Incorporating** a co-op association or company |
| Owner holds | **Fee simple** in the lot + share of CP as tenant in common | **Shares** + a long-term **lease** of a unit |
| Governed by | *Strata Property Act*, regulations, bylaws, rules | *Cooperative Association Act*, its rules, possibly a shareholders' agreement |
| Selling | Transfer the fee simple; buyer can **mortgage** it; **few restrictions** on who can buy | Transfer shares and assign the lease; **board approval** usually needed; **financing harder** |

## Two alerts

> 🚨 **ALERT 1: Read the documents, then read them again.** Minutes, depreciation reports and Form B are where special levies, envelope problems, lawsuits, insurance deductibles and new bylaws show up. Licensees have been disciplined for skipping them, and buyers who don't read them may lose their claims (*Sask v. Brooke*).

> 🚨 **ALERT 2: Never assume a strata's restrictions.** Age, pet, short-term rental and use bylaws vary, and change. Check the **current filed bylaws** and the **recent minutes** for amendments passed but not yet filed (Form B lists these). Don't rely on what the seller, another agent or the building's appearance suggests.

## Trap list: things the exam likes to twist

1. *Strata Property Act* replaced the *Condominium Act* on **July 1, 2000**; Standard Bylaws apply to all stratas from **Jan 1, 2002**.
2. **Unit entitlement** sets both ownership of CP and share of expenses (**Form V**). Formula: UE ÷ total UE × expense.
3. Changing the expense formula needs a **unanimous** vote, **registered**.
4. **LCP** runs with the **lot**; short term exclusive use is personal, max **1 year** (5 for EV stalls).
5. LCP by **¾ vote** resolution (filed; in the Common Property Record) or **unanimous** strata plan amendment.
6. EV-related approvals drop to a **majority**; strata must decide an EV request within **3 months** and can't unreasonably refuse.
7. A judgment against the strata is a judgment against **every owner**, by unit entitlement.
8. Strata insurance: **full replacement value** on CP and developer-installed fixtures, plus liability. Not owners' contents or deductibles passed on.
9. Records: copies within **2 weeks**; bylaws and rules within **1 week**.
10. A tenant on a **3+ year** lease is automatically assigned the owner's rights (after written notice); otherwise the owner assigns by written notice (s.147).
11. **Form B**: **8 days**, **$35** max; attaches rules, budget, depreciation report (and electrical planning report).
12. **Form F**: **1 week**, **$15** max, valid **60 days**, needed to register a transfer (not court-ordered sales). Can include fines; not unproven claims.
13. Through the seller's authorization: **no inspection fee**, copies **25¢/page**.
14. *Sask v. Brooke*: documents were available → **no reasonable reliance**.
15. First AGM: within **6 weeks** of **50% + 1** lots conveyed or **9 months** after first conveyance, whichever is first. Penalty **$1,000** per 30 days, then per 7 days.
16. **20 days'** notice of general meetings; AGM within **2 months** of fiscal year-end; **20%** of votes can demand an SGM.
17. Mortgagee voting: right in the mortgage + **3 days'** notice; only insurance, maintenance, finance and security matters. Voting age **16**.
18. Strata managers must be **licensed**; act for the **corporation**, instructed by **council**.
19. Operating fund: **≥ once a year**; CRF: **< once a year**. CRF minimum **10%** of operating budget. No CRF refund on sale.
20. Council's default unapproved spending: lesser of **5%** or **$2,000**.
21. Interim budget shortfall > **10%** → **double or triple** penalty; refunds of **$100 or less** can go to the CRF.
22. CRF seed: lesser of **10% × years** or **50%** of interim operating expenses.
23. Special levy: **¾** by unit entitlement; **unanimous** otherwise; failed ¾ for safety repairs → majority + court within **90 days**.
24. Levy instalments: whoever owns the lot **when each payment is due** (s.109). Holdbacks use **s.108** wording.
25. Depreciation report every **5 years**; **30-year** forecast; exempt under **5 lots**.
26. **2 weeks'** demand before collecting. Liens for fees and levies, **not fines**. Strata lien beats mortgages, but not builders liens, Crown liens or judgment-share liens.
27. Rules cover **CP only**, need **majority ratification** at the next AGM; bylaws beat rules; amended bylaws need **LTO filing**.
28. Residential bylaw amendment: **unanimous** before the 2nd AGM, **¾** after. Mixed: ¾ of **each** group.
29. Standard fines **$50/$10**; regulation max **$200/$50** every **7 days**; short-term rentals **$1,000 daily**.
30. **No rental restrictions** since **Nov 24, 2022** (short-term rental bans OK). **Form K** to the strata within **2 weeks**.
31. Age bylaws: **55+ only**; *Stevens* fines upheld.
32. Standard pet bylaw: **one dog or one cat**, **2 caged birds**; guide and service dogs always allowed; existing pets grandfathered.
33. Strata always repairs **structure, exterior, balconies, exterior windows/doors, fences and railings**, even on LCP.
34. Winding up: **80%** + **court order**; hiring a brokerage to market: **majority** (*Dubas*).
35. Leasehold: ground lease **≥ 50 years**; 1 year's notice or automatic **5-year** renewal; **no HBRP**.
36. Co-op: shares + lease; **board approval** of buyers; harder financing; **HBRP applies** to co-op interests with a right to occupy.

## Drill

**Q1.** A strata has a total unit entitlement of 12,000. Your client's lot has 96. The owners approve a $450,000 special levy by unit entitlement. What's your client's share, and what vote was needed?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

96 ÷ 12,000 × $450,000 = **$3,600**. Sharing by unit entitlement needs a **¾ vote**. (Any other sharing method would need a **unanimous** vote.)

</details>

**Q2.** Your buyer's offer on a condo is accepted, subject to strata documents. What do you get, and what do you look for?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

- **Form B** (Information Certificate, 8 days, ≤ $35) with the **rules**, **budget** and **depreciation report** attached; plus at least **2 years** of **council, AGM and SGM minutes** (and section minutes), **financial statements**, and current **bylaws**, via the seller's written authorization.
- Look for: approved or proposed **special levies**; notices of upcoming ¾/80%/unanimous votes; **CRF** balance vs depreciation report needs; **envelope, roof, plumbing or elevator** issues; **lawsuits** and judgments; **work orders**; **insurance** coverage and **deductibles**; **bylaws** on pets, age, short-term rentals; **unfiled amendments**; **parking/storage** allocations; any **alteration indemnities** for the unit.
- Leave enough time for the subject (records within 2 weeks), and make sure your client actually **reads** them (*Sask v. Brooke*).

</details>

**Q3.** A special levy is approved on May 1, payable in two instalments: June 1 and September 1. Completion is July 15. Who pays what?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

Under **s.109**, the owner when each payment is due pays it. The **seller** pays the **June 1** instalment; the **buyer** (owner after July 15) pays the **September 1** instalment, unless the contract says otherwise. A buyer's licensee would normally negotiate a clause making the seller responsible for the whole levy (Knowledge Base wording), or a **holdback** using **s.108** wording.

</details>

**Q4.** An owner owes $4,000 in bylaw fines and is selling. Can the strata register a lien? How else can it get paid?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**No lien** for fines (or bylaw-remedy costs). The strata can **sue** or go to the **Civil Resolution Tribunal** (within the 2-year limitation period) and register a judgment. Or, since the seller needs a **Form F** to transfer title, the strata can **withhold the Certificate of Payment** until the fines are paid.

</details>

**Q5.** Your buyer is 48 and wants a unit in a building with a 55+ age bylaw. The seller says, "Don't worry, lots of younger people live here." What do you do?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

Don't rely on the seller, signage or who seems to live there. Get the **current bylaws** and **recent minutes**. If a valid 55+ bylaw exists, your buyer generally **can't live there** (younger residents may be grandfathered, caregivers, or family of qualifying residents). Enforcement can be costly (*Stevens*: $13,000+ in fines). Explain this clearly, and if they have questions about enforceability or exemptions, refer them to a **lawyer**.

</details>

**Q6.** A buyer is excited about a "bargain" leasehold strata unit with 31 years left on its lease. What do you need to discuss?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

- They're buying a **leasehold interest** (by assignment), not fee simple. It's a **wasting asset**: value declines as the term runs down.
- Get the **strata lot lease** and check for a **Schedule of Restrictions**; ask about **rent** (prepaid or ongoing, rent reviews).
- At expiry the **landlord** decides: renew (min 5 years, new rent) or buy out at a value that may **exclude the land**; this is legally unsettled.
- **Financing** may be harder; check lender requirements.
- **No HBRP** for leasehold.
- Strongly recommend **legal advice**, and decline or get help if you lack the expertise.

</details>

## Mindset: buying a strata means joining a corporation

A strata buyer isn't just buying walls. They're becoming a **member of a small corporation** with shared debts, shared decisions and shared problems. Your job is to make sure they know what they're joining: its **finances**, its **rules**, its **condition** and its **plans**.

That means reading what's dull: minutes, budgets, depreciation reports. That's where the money is. A one-line minute note ("council will seek quotes for envelope remediation") can be worth more to your client than anything in the listing.

> 🏢 **For commercial specifically.** Strata isn't just condos.
>
> - **Commercial strata** (retail, office, industrial and warehouse units) is a big market. Know the **non-residential** bylaw rules (¾ or as the bylaws say), and how **sections** let commercial owners avoid paying for residential services.
> - **Mixed-use** buildings need ¾ approval from **each** group for bylaw changes, so commercial owners have real voting power.
> - **Use restrictions** matter enormously to commercial buyers: check bylaws for permitted uses, signage, hours, deliveries and noise, and remember bylaws can change after purchase (*Kunzler*).
> - **Strata wind-ups** (80% + court order) are **land assembly** opportunities: hiring a brokerage takes only a majority (*Dubas*).
> - **Leasehold** land (First Nations, universities, municipalities) is common for commercial and industrial development. Understand ground leases, rent reviews and what happens at expiry.

> **This week's habit.** Find a real strata's Form B or a depreciation report online (many are posted with listings). Read the executive summary and the 30-year CRF forecast. Ask: Is the reserve fund keeping up? When is the next big expense? What would a buyer's share be?
